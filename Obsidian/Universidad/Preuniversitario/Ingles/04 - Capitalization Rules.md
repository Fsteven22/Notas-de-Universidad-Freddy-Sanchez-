# 🎯 Capitalization Rules

## 🌟 What Is Capitalization?

> [!info] Definition **Capitalization means writing a letter in its uppercase (capital) form instead of lowercase (small) form. English has specific rules about when to use capital letters. Proper capitalization makes writing clear, professional, and easier to read.**
> 
> **Key points:**
> 
> - **Uppercase letters:** A, B, C, D, E, F, G... (also called "capital letters" or "majuscules")
> - **Lowercase letters:** a, b, c, d, e, f, g... (also called "small letters" or "minuscules")
> - Capital letters signal **important information** (beginnings, names, titles)
> - Incorrect capitalization can **change meaning** or look unprofessional
> - English capitalizes **more** than Spanish does
> - Rules are specific and must be memorized

### 📖 Historical Context

> [!note]- Development of Capitalization Rules
> 
> **Ancient origins (3000 BC - 800 AD):**
> 
> - **Early writing systems:** Only one letter form (no capitals vs lowercase)
> - **Roman inscriptions:** All UPPERCASE on monuments (SPQR, etc.)
> - **Papyrus and scrolls:** All lowercase for everyday writing
> - **No spaces between words:** DIFFICULTTOREAD or difficulttoread
> 
> **Medieval period (800-1500 AD):**
> 
> - **Carolingian minuscule (~800 AD):** First clear lowercase letters developed
> - **Illuminated manuscripts:** Large decorated capital at beginning of texts
> - **Majuscule vs minuscule:** Two letter forms now distinguished
> - **Capitals for emphasis:** Important words, names, religious terms
> 
> **Early printing (1450-1700):**
> 
> - **Gutenberg Bible (1455):** Standardized capital usage
> - **Excessive capitalization:** English capitalized MANY nouns (like German)
> - **17th century style:** "The King of England visited the City of London"
> - **Emphasis and respect:** Important words and concepts got capitals
> 
> **Modern standardization (1700-present):**
> 
> - **18th century reform:** Reduced capitalization in English
> - **American vs British differences:** Some variations developed
> - **20th century rules:** Current system mostly established
> - **Digital age:** New challenges (email, internet, brand names)

## 📋 The Essential Capitalization Rules

### 1. ✅ Beginning of Sentences

> [!success]- Rule 1: First Word of Every Sentence
> 
> **Always capitalize the first word of a sentence.**
> 
> **Examples:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ The cat is sleeping.
> ✗ the cat is sleeping.
> 
> ✓ She loves to read books.
> ✗ she loves to read books.
> 
> ✓ Where are you going?
> ✗ where are you going?
> 
> ✓ Please help me with this.
> ✗ please help me with this.
> ```
> 
> **After periods, question marks, and exclamation points:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ I went to the store. Then I came home.
> ✓ Are you ready? Let's go!
> ✓ Stop! Don't do that.
> ```
> 
> **After colons (when introducing complete sentences):**
> 
> ```
> American style (more common):
> ✓ Remember this: Always be kind to others.
> 
> British style (also acceptable):
> ✓ Remember this: always be kind to others.
> 
> Note: If not a complete sentence, no capital:
> ✓ You need three things: bread, milk, and eggs.
> ```

---

### 2. 👤 The Pronoun "I"

> [!success]- Rule 2: Always Capitalize "I"
> 
> **The pronoun "I" is ALWAYS capitalized, no matter where it appears in the sentence.**
> 
> **Why?**
> 
> ```
> Historical reasons: In Middle English, "I" was written as "ich"
> When shortened to just "i", it looked too small and got lost
> Solution: Always capitalize it for visibility
> Result: It became a unique rule in English
> ```
> 
> **Examples:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ I am a student.
> ✗ i am a student.
> 
> ✓ My friend and I went shopping.
> ✗ My friend and i went shopping.
> 
> ✓ She asked if I was ready.
> ✗ She asked if i was ready.
> 
> ✓ Yesterday I saw a movie.
> ✗ Yesterday i saw a movie.
> ```
> 
> **Contractions with "I":**
> 
> ```
> ✓ I'm, I'll, I've, I'd
> ✗ i'm, i'll, i've, i'd
> ```
> 
> **Comparison with Spanish:**
> 
> ```
> Spanish: yo (lowercase unless at beginning)
> English: I (ALWAYS uppercase)
> ```

### 3. 🏷️ Proper Nouns

> [!success]- Rule 3: Names of Specific People, Places, and Things
> 
> **Definition:** A proper noun names a SPECIFIC person, place, organization, or thing. Always capitalize proper nouns.
> 
> **A. People's names:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ Maria, John, Shakespeare, Dr. Smith
> ✗ maria, john, shakespeare, dr. smith
> 
> Full names:
> ✓ María García López
> ✓ William Shakespeare
> ✓ Martin Luther King Jr.
> 
> Titles with names:
> ✓ President Biden
> ✓ Queen Elizabeth
> ✓ Professor Johnson
> ✓ Captain America
> ```
> 
> **B. Places:**
> 
> ```
> Cities:
> ✓ Guayaquil, London, New York, Tokyo
> ✗ guayaquil, london, new york, tokyo
> 
> Countries:
> ✓ Ecuador, United States, Japan, France
> ✗ ecuador, united states, japan, france
> 
> States/Provinces:
> ✓ California, Guayas, Ontario, Bavaria
> 
> Continents:
> ✓ South America, Europe, Asia, Africa
> 
> Mountains, Rivers, Oceans:
> ✓ Mount Everest, Amazon River, Pacific Ocean
> ✓ The Andes, Lake Titicaca, Mediterranean Sea
> 
> Specific regions:
> ✓ the Middle East, the South, the West Coast
> ✓ Latin America, Southeast Asia
> 
> BUT general directions (not regions):
> ✓ Go north on Main Street. (direction, lowercase)
> ✓ I live in the North. (region, uppercase)
> ```
> 
> **C. Organizations and institutions:**
> 
> ```
> Companies:
> ✓ Google, Microsoft, Coca-Cola, Amazon
> 
> Schools and universities:
> ✓ Harvard University, Central High School
> ✓ Universidad de Guayaquil
> 
> Government bodies:
> ✓ United Nations, Congress, Parliament
> ✓ Supreme Court, Senate
> 
> Organizations:
> ✓ World Health Organization (WHO)
> ✓ North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO)
> ```
> 
> **D. Specific buildings and monuments:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ Eiffel Tower, Statue of Liberty
> ✓ Empire State Building, Golden Gate Bridge
> ✓ Taj Mahal, Great Wall of China
> 
> BUT general terms lowercase:
> ✓ the tower, a bridge, the building
> ```
> 
> **E. Brand names:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ iPhone, Coca-Cola, Nike, Toyota
> ✓ McDonald's, Starbucks, Facebook
> 
> Note: Some brands have unusual capitalization (respect their style):
> ✓ iPhone, eBay, iPad (not Iphone, Ebay, Ipad)
> ```

### 4. 📅 Days, Months, and Holidays

> [!success]- Rule 4: Calendar Terms
> 
> **Days of the week:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday
> ✓ Friday, Saturday, Sunday
> ✗ monday, tuesday, wednesday
> 
> Examples:
> ✓ I have class on Monday.
> ✓ Let's meet next Friday.
> ```
> 
> **Months of the year:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ January, February, March, April, May, June
> ✓ July, August, September, October, November, December
> ✗ january, february, march
> 
> Examples:
> ✓ My birthday is in July.
> ✓ She was born in December 1995.
> ```
> 
> **Holidays and special observances:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ Christmas, Easter, Hanukkah, Ramadan
> ✓ New Year's Day, Independence Day
> ✓ Thanksgiving, Halloween, Valentine's Day
> ✓ Mother's Day, Father's Day
> 
> Examples:
> ✓ We celebrate Christmas in December.
> ✓ Happy New Year!
> ```
> 
> **Important comparison with Spanish:**
> 
> ```
> English: Monday, January (ALWAYS capitalized)
> Spanish: lunes, enero (lowercase)
> 
> This is a MAJOR difference!
> ```
> 
> **BUT seasons are lowercase:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ spring, summer, fall/autumn, winter
> ✗ Spring, Summer, Fall, Winter
> 
> Exception: When personified or in titles:
> ✓ Old Man Winter
> ✓ "Winter is Coming" (Game of Thrones)
> ```

### 6. 📚 Titles of Works

> [!success]- Rule 6: Books, Movies, Songs, and Other Creative Works
> 
> **General rule: Capitalize the first word, last word, and all important words.**
> 
> **What to capitalize:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ First word (always)
> ✓ Last word (always)
> ✓ Nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs
> ✓ Subordinating conjunctions (if, because, that, etc.)
> ```
> 
> **What NOT to capitalize (unless first or last word):**
> 
> ```
> ✗ Articles: a, an, the
> ✗ Coordinating conjunctions: and, but, or, nor, for, so, yet
> ✗ Prepositions: in, on, at, to, by, for, with, from (usually)
> ```
> 
> **Books:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ The Great Gatsby
> ✓ One Hundred Years of Solitude
> ✓ Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone
> ✗ Harry Potter And The Philosopher's Stone (wrong!)
> ```
> 
> **Movies and TV shows:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ The Lord of the Rings
> ✓ Breaking Bad
> ✓ The Silence of the Lambs
> ```
> 
> **Songs:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ "Let It Be"
> ✓ "Shape of You"
> ✓ "I Will Always Love You"
> ```
> 
> **Articles and essays:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ "The Importance of Being Earnest"
> ✓ "How to Win Friends and Influence People"
> ```
> 
> **Magazines and newspapers:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ The New York Times
> ✓ National Geographic
> ✓ Time Magazine
> ```
> 
> **Note about prepositions:**
> 
> ```
> Short prepositions (3 letters or fewer): usually lowercase
> ✓ "The Cat in the Hat"
> 
> Long prepositions (4+ letters): often capitalized
> ✓ "Looking Through the Glass"
> 
> But first/last word is ALWAYS capitalized:
> ✓ "To Kill a Mockingbird" (To is first word)
> ✓ "The Road Not Taken" (not usually capitalized, but accepted)
> ```

### 7. 🎓 Titles and Positions

> [!success]- Rule 7: Professional and Social Titles
> 
> **Titles BEFORE names: Capitalize**
> 
> ```
> ✓ President Lincoln
> ✓ Dr. Smith
> ✓ Professor Johnson
> ✓ Captain America
> ✓ Queen Elizabeth
> ✓ Pope Francis
> ✓ Senator Martinez
> ✓ Judge Brown
> 
> Examples:
> ✓ I spoke with Doctor Martinez yesterday.
> ✓ President Biden gave a speech.
> ```
> 
> **Titles AFTER names or used alone: Usually lowercase**
> 
> ```
> ✓ Abraham Lincoln, president of the United States
> ✓ John Smith, doctor at Central Hospital
> ✓ The professor is in his office.
> ✓ My uncle is a captain in the navy.
> 
> Examples:
> ✓ The president gave a speech. (generic reference)
> ✓ She is a professor at the university.
> ✓ He works as a doctor.
> ```
> 
> **Exception: Very high offices can be capitalized when referring to specific person:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ The President will address the nation. (President of the United States)
> ✓ The Pope visited Ecuador. (specific current Pope)
> ✓ The Queen celebrated her jubilee. (specific Queen)
> 
> BUT:
> ✓ The president of the company arrived. (lowercase for company president)
> ✓ Every queen in history... (generic, lowercase)
> ```
> 
> **Abbreviations of titles:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ Dr., Mr., Mrs., Ms., Prof., Gen., Rev., Hon.
> 
> Always capitalize abbreviations:
> ✓ Dr. Smith
> ✓ Mr. Johnson
> ✓ Prof. Garcia
> ```
> 
> **Family titles:**
> 
> ```
> Used as names (replacing the person's name): Capitalize
> ✓ I asked Mom if I could go.
> ✓ Can Dad help me?
> ✓ Tell Grandma I said hello.
> 
> Used with possessive or article: Lowercase
> ✓ I asked my mom if I could go.
> ✓ Can your dad help me?
> ✓ Tell my grandma I said hello.
> ```

### 8. 🗺️ Geographic Terms

> [!success]- Rule 8: Compass Directions and Regions
> 
> **Specific regions (proper nouns): Capitalize**
> 
> ```
> ✓ the South (southern United States)
> ✓ the Midwest (region of US)
> ✓ the Middle East (region)
> ✓ Latin America (region)
> ✓ Southeast Asia (region)
> ✓ the West Coast (specific coast)
> 
> Examples:
> ✓ I live in the South.
> ✓ She moved to the West.
> ✓ The North won the Civil War.
> ```
> 
> **Directions (not specific regions): Lowercase**
> 
> ```
> ✓ Go north on Main Street. (direction)
> ✓ The bird flew south for winter. (direction)
> ✓ Turn east at the light. (direction)
> ✓ They live west of here. (direction)
> 
> Examples:
> ✓ Drive three miles east.
> ✓ The sun rises in the east.
> ✓ Head south until you see the sign.
> ```
> 
> **Adjectives from regions:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ Southern hospitality
> ✓ Western culture
> ✓ Northern lights
> ✓ Eastern philosophy
> 
> BUT directional adjectives: lowercase
> ✓ the southern part of the city
> ✓ the western border
> ```

### 9. 📖 Quotations

> [!success]- Rule 9: First Word of Direct Quotes
> 
> **Capitalize the first word of a direct quotation when it's a complete sentence:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ She said, "The meeting starts at noon."
> ✗ She said, "the meeting starts at noon."
> 
> ✓ He asked, "Where are you going?"
> ✓ "I love this place," she exclaimed.
> ```
> 
> **If the quote is split, don't capitalize the second part:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ "I think," she said, "we should leave now."
> ✗ "I think," she said, "We should leave now."
> 
> ✓ "The book is good," he noted, "but a bit long."
> ```
> 
> **If the quote continues after interruption as new sentence, capitalize:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ "I agree," she said. "We need to act quickly."
> (Two separate sentences)
> ```
> 
> **Partial quotes (not complete sentences): Don't capitalize**
> 
> ```
> ✓ He said the movie was "absolutely incredible."
> ✗ He said the movie was "Absolutely incredible."
> 
> ✓ She called it "the worst decision ever made."
> ```

## ⚠️ Common Capitalization Mistakes

> [!warning]- Errors to Avoid
> 
> **Mistake 1: Capitalizing common nouns**
> 
> ```
> ✗ I went to the Store. → ✓ I went to the store.
> ✗ My Sister is a Teacher. → ✓ My sister is a teacher.
> ✗ The Dog is sleeping. → ✓ The dog is sleeping.
> 
> Remember: Only capitalize SPECIFIC names, not general categories.
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 2: Not capitalizing proper nouns**
> 
> ```
> ✗ i live in guayaquil, ecuador. → ✓ I live in Guayaquil, Ecuador.
> ✗ she speaks spanish and english. → ✓ She speaks Spanish and English.
> ✗ we celebrate christmas. → ✓ We celebrate Christmas.
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 3: Capitalizing seasons**
> 
> ```
> ✗ I love Spring. → ✓ I love spring.
> ✗ Summer is hot. → ✓ Summer is hot.
> ✗ They met in Winter. → ✓ They met in winter.
> 
> Exception: Spring Break, Winter Olympics (proper nouns)
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 4: Wrong capitalization in titles**
> 
> ```
> ✗ The Lord Of The Rings → ✓ The Lord of the Rings
> ✗ Harry Potter And The Chamber Of Secrets → ✓ Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets
> ✗ to kill a mockingbird → ✓ To Kill a Mockingbird
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 5: Capitalizing after semicolons**
> 
> ```
> ✗ I like tea; She prefers coffee. → ✓ I like tea; she prefers coffee.
> 
> (Semicolon connects two related sentences; don't capitalize after it)
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 6: Lowercase "I"**
> 
> ```
> ✗ My friend and i went shopping. → ✓ My friend and I went shopping.
> ✗ i think it's great. → ✓ I think it's great.
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 7: Capitalizing school subjects (except languages)**
> 
> ```
> ✗ I study Mathematics and History. → ✓ I study mathematics and history.
> ✓ I study English and Spanish. (languages always capitalized)
> ✓ I'm taking Math 101. (specific course name)
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 8: Days and months in Spanish style**
> 
> ```
> ✗ monday, january → ✓ Monday, January
> 
> Remember: English ALWAYS capitalizes days and months!
> ```

## 💡 Special Cases and Exceptions

> [!info]- Advanced Capitalization Rules
> 
> **1. After colons:**
> 
> ```
> American style: Capitalize if complete sentence follows
> ✓ Remember this: Always check your work.
> 
> British style: Usually lowercase
> ✓ Remember this: always check your work.
> 
> Lists after colons: lowercase
> ✓ Bring three items: pen, paper, and calculator.
> ```
> 
> **2. Hyphenated words in titles:**
> 
> ```
> Capitalize both parts if both are important words:
> ✓ "The Nineteenth-Century Novel"
> ✓ "Well-Known Facts"
> 
> But not small words after hyphen:
> ✓ "Two-thirds of the Population"
> ✓ "Run-of-the-mill Product"
> ```
> 
> **3. Website and email addresses:**
> 
> ```
> Usually all lowercase:
> ✓ www.example.com
> ✓ user@email.com
> 
> Capitalize in regular text when starting sentence:
> ✓ Google.com is a search engine.
> But URL itself: google.com
> ```
> 
> **4. Historical periods and events:**
> 
> ```
> Capitalize specific periods/events:
> ✓ the Renaissance
> ✓ the Industrial Revolution
> ✓ World War II
> ✓ the Great Depression
> ✓ the Middle Ages
> 
> But general terms lowercase:
> ✓ the nineteenth century
> ✓ the colonial period
> ✓ the modern era
> ```
> 
> **5. After em dash:**
> 
> ```
> Don't capitalize after em dash (—):
> ✓ I wanted to go—really, I did—but I was too tired.
> ```
> 
> **6. Brand names that break rules:**
> 
> ```
> Respect company's chosen style:
> ✓ iPhone (not IPhone or Iphone)
> ✓ eBay (not EBay or Ebay)
> ✓ iPad (not IPad or Ipad)
> ✓ McDonald's (not Mc Donald's)
> ```

> Conexión: La capitalización se aplica especialmente a:
> 
> NOUNS (Sustantivos):
> • Common nouns → lowercase (dog, city, teacher)
> • Proper nouns → CAPITALIZE (Spot, London, Mr. Smith)
> 
> PRONOUNS (Pronombres):
> • "I" → ALWAYS capitalized
> • All others → lowercase (unless starting sentence)
> 
> VERBS, ADJECTIVES, ADVERBS:
> • Capitalize in titles of works
> • "The Dog Ran Quickly" (title style)
> 
> INTERJECTIONS:
> • At beginning of sentence → capitalize
> • "Wow! That's amazing!"
> ```
> 
> 
> **[[03 - Parts of a Sentence]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión: La capitalización es fundamental para la estructura de oraciones:
> 
> • Primera palabra de cada SENTENCE → siempre mayúscula
> • SUBJECT (sujeto) puede ser un proper noun → capitalizar
> • PREDICATE contiene verbos que pueden estar en títulos → capitalizar
> 
> Ejemplo integrado:
> "Maria and I went to London last Monday."
> 
> Análisis de Nota 04 (actual):
> - Maria = proper noun (capitalized)
> - I = pronoun (always capitalized)
> - London = proper noun - place (capitalized)
> - Monday = day of week (capitalized)
> 
> Análisis de Nota 03 (siguiente):
> - Maria and I = compound subject
> - went to London last Monday = predicate
> ```
> 
> **[[05 - Punctuation Rules]]** (tema futuro sugerido)
> 
> ```
> Conexión: La capitalización trabaja junto con la puntuación:
> 
> • Después de punto (.) → nueva oración, capitalizar
> • Después de punto de interrogación (?) → capitalizar
> • Después de punto de exclamación (!) → capitalizar
> • Después de dos puntos (:) → a veces capitalizar
> • Después de punto y coma (;) → NO capitalizar
> 
> Comillas y capitalización:
> • Citas directas → capitalizar primera palabra
> • Títulos de obras → reglas especiales de capitalización
> ```
> 
> **[[06 - Writing Formal Documents]]** (tema futuro sugerido)
> 
> ```
> • Cartas formales: capitalizaciones especiales
> • Emails profesionales: cuando usar mayúsculas
> • Ensayos académicos: títulos, referencias
> • Reportes: encabezados, secciones
> ```
> 
> **Aplicación práctica con notas anteriores:**
> 
> **Conexión con [[01 - Regular and Irregular Verbs]]:**
> 
> ```
> Los verbos se capitalizan cuando:
> • Empiezan una oración: "Walked to school yesterday."
> • Están en títulos: "Gone with the Wind"
> • Son parte de nombres propios: "the United States"
> 
> Pero NO se capitalizan normalmente:
> • En medio de oración: "I walked yesterday."
> ```
> 
> **Conexión con [[02 - Parts of Speech (POS)]]:**
> 
> ```
> Cada parte de la oración tiene reglas de capitalización:
> 
> NOUNS: Proper nouns → capitalize
> PRONOUNS: Only "I" → always capitalize
> VERBS: In titles → capitalize
> ADJECTIVES: From proper nouns → capitalize (American, Spanish)
> ADVERBS: In titles → capitalize
> PREPOSITIONS: In titles → usually lowercase
> CONJUNCTIONS: In titles → usually lowercase
> INTERJECTIONS: Beginning of sentence → capitalize
> ```

## 🎓 Study Tips and Memory Aids

> [!tip]- How to Master Capitalization
> 
> **1. The "MINT" acronym for always capitalize:**
> 
> ```
> M = Months and Days (Monday, January)
> I = "I" pronoun (always!)
> N = Names (Maria, London, NASA)
> T = Titles and first words (The Great Gatsby, "Hello.")
> ```
> 
> **2. Remember the key differences from Spanish:**
> 
> ```
> English capitalizes MORE than Spanish:
> 
> ✓ Days: Monday (English) vs lunes (Spanish)
> ✓ Months: January (English) vs enero (Spanish)
> ✓ Languages: English (English) vs inglés (Spanish)
> ✓ Nationalities: American (English) vs americano (Spanish)
> 
> This is the BIGGEST challenge for Spanish speakers!
> ```
> 
> **3. The "When in doubt" rule:**
> 
> ```
> Ask yourself:
> 1. Is it a SPECIFIC name? → Capitalize
> 2. Is it a GENERAL category? → Lowercase
> 
> Examples:
> - my mother (general) vs Mother Teresa (specific name)
> - a president (general) vs President Lincoln (specific)
> - the city (general) vs New York City (specific)
> ```
> 
> **4. Title capitalization shortcut:**
> 
> ```
> Remember: "Articles, Coordinating conjunctions, and Short prepositions"
> 
> DON'T capitalize (unless first/last):
> - a, an, the (articles)
> - and, but, or, nor, for, so, yet (FANBOYS)
> - in, on, at, to, by, for (short prepositions)
> 
> DO capitalize everything else!
> ```
> 
> **5. Practice daily:**
> 
> ```
> • Write 5 sentences daily focusing on capitalization
> • Read English texts and notice capital letters
> • Edit your own writing specifically for capitals
> • Use spell-check, but understand the rules yourself
> ```
