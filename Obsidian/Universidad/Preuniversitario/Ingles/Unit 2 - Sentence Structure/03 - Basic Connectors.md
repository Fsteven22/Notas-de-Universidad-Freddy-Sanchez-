# 🎯 Basic Connectors (Linking Words)

## 🌟 What Are Connectors?

> [!info] Definition **Connectors (also called linking words, conjunctions, or discourse markers) are words or phrases that link ideas, sentences, or paragraphs together. They show relationships between ideas, making your English flow naturally and helping readers understand how your thoughts connect.**
> 
> **Key points:**
> 
> - **Purpose:** Connect ideas and show relationships between them
> - **Types:** Addition, contrast, cause/effect, sequence, examples
> - **Function:** Make writing and speech coherent and logical
> - **Importance:** Transform simple sentences into complex, sophisticated communication
> - **Positioning:** Can appear at the beginning, middle, or between sentences
> - Essential for clear, natural-sounding English

### 📖 Historical Context

> [!note]- Development of English Connectors
> 
> **Old English period (450-1100 AD):**
> 
> - **Simple connectors:** Mainly "and" (ond/and), "but" (ac), "or" (oþþe)
> - **Limited variety:** Fewer connecting words than modern English
> - **Parataxis preference:** Simple sentences placed side by side without connectors
> - **Example:** "He came. He saw. He conquered." (simple coordination)
> - **"And" was dominant:** Used extensively for all types of connections
> 
> **Middle English period (1100-1500):**
> 
> - **French influence:** Many new connectors borrowed from Norman French
>     - "because" (from French "par cause")
>     - "therefore" (from French influence on structure)
> - **Latin influence:** Through church and scholarship
>     - "however," "moreover," "nevertheless"
> - **Greater complexity:** More sophisticated sentence structures develop
> - **Subordination increases:** More use of complex sentences with clauses
> 
> **Early Modern English (1500-1700):**
> 
> - **Expansion of connectors:** More precise relationship markers
> - **Shakespeare's era:** Creative use of connectors for dramatic effect
> - **Printing press impact:** Standardization of connector usage
> - **Latin-based connectors:** "consequently," "furthermore," "although"
> - **Academic writing develops:** Need for precise logical connections
> 
> **Modern English (1700-present):**
> 
> - **Rich variety:** Hundreds of connectors for subtle distinctions
> - **Formal vs. informal:** Different connectors for different contexts
>     - Formal: "furthermore," "nevertheless," "consequently"
>     - Informal: "plus," "anyway," "so"
> - **Register awareness:** Understanding when to use which connector
> - **Academic conventions:** Specific connectors preferred in formal writing
> - **Spoken vs. written:** Different connector patterns emerge

## 🔗 Types of Connectors

### ➕ Addition Connectors

> [!success]- Adding Information
> 
> **AND - The most basic connector:**
> 
> ```
> Function: Connects similar or related ideas
> Position: Between words, phrases, or clauses
> 
> Examples:
> • I like tea and coffee.
> • She is smart and funny.
> • He works hard and studies every day.
> • We need bread, milk, and eggs. (list)
> 
> With sentences:
> • I went to the store. And I bought some milk. (informal)
> • Better: I went to the store and bought some milk.
> ```
> 
> **ALSO - Adds similar information:**
> 
> ```
> Position: Usually mid-sentence, before main verb or after BE
> 
> Examples:
> • I like tea. I also like coffee.
> • She speaks English. She also speaks Spanish.
> • He is smart. He is also hardworking.
> 
> Position rules:
> • After BE: She is also intelligent.
> • Before other verbs: I also play guitar.
> • Can start sentence (formal): Also, we need to consider...
> ```
> 
> **TOO - Adds agreement (informal):**
> 
> ```
> Position: End of sentence or clause
> 
> Examples:
> • I like pizza. I like pasta, too.
> • She's coming to the party, too.
> • He speaks French, and he speaks German, too.
> 
> Note: More informal than "also"
> Usually with positive statements
> ```
> 
> **AS WELL - Similar to "too" (slightly more formal):**
> 
> ```
> Position: End of sentence
> 
> Examples:
> • I play tennis. I play basketball as well.
> • She works here as well.
> • They're coming as well.
> 
> Note: More common in British English
> ```
> 
> **IN ADDITION / ADDITIONALLY - Formal addition:**
> 
> ```
> Position: Beginning of sentence (usually)
> Use: Formal writing, academic contexts
> 
> Examples:
> • I speak English. In addition, I speak Spanish.
> • The hotel has a pool. In addition, it has a gym.
> • Additionally, we offer free breakfast.
> 
> Note: More formal than "also" or "too"
> ```
> 
> **FURTHERMORE / MOREOVER - Very formal addition:**
> 
> ```
> Position: Beginning of sentence
> Use: Academic writing, formal speeches
> 
> Examples:
> • The plan is expensive. Furthermore, it's impractical.
> • We need more time. Moreover, we need more resources.
> 
> Note: Very formal, rare in conversation
> ```
> 
> **PLUS - Informal addition:**
> 
> ```
> Position: Beginning of sentence (spoken English)
> Use: Casual conversation
> 
> Examples:
> • I don't want to go. Plus, I'm tired.
> • The food is good. Plus, it's cheap.
> 
> Note: Very informal, avoid in formal writing
> ```

### ⚡ Contrast Connectors

> [!warning]- Showing Differences or Opposition
> 
> **BUT - Basic contrast:**
> 
> ```
> Function: Shows difference or unexpected result
> Position: Between clauses
> 
> Examples:
> • I like tea, but I don't like coffee.
> • She's smart, but she's lazy.
> • It's expensive, but it's worth it.
> • He tried hard, but he failed.
> 
> Note: Can start sentence in informal speech
> • But I don't understand! (informal)
> ```
> 
> **HOWEVER - Formal contrast:**
> 
> ```
> Position: Beginning of sentence, after semicolon, or mid-sentence
> Use: More formal than "but"
> 
> Examples:
> • I like tea. However, I don't like coffee.
> • She studied hard; however, she failed the test.
> • The plan, however, was rejected.
> 
> Punctuation:
> • Beginning: However, + comma
> • Middle: , however,
> • After semicolon: ; however,
> ```
> 
> **ALTHOUGH / THOUGH / EVEN THOUGH - Concession:**
> 
> ```
> Function: Introduces a contrasting clause
> Position: Beginning or middle of sentence
> 
> Although (formal):
> • Although it was raining, we went out.
> • We went out, although it was raining.
> • Although he's rich, he's not happy.
> 
> Though (less formal):
> • Though it was late, she called.
> • She called, though it was late.
> 
> Even though (emphasis):
> • Even though I'm tired, I'll help you.
> • I'll help you, even though I'm tired.
> 
> Note: These introduce dependent clauses, need main clause
> ```
> 
> **YET - Contrast with surprise:**
> 
> ```
> Function: Similar to "but," shows unexpected result
> Position: Between clauses
> 
> Examples:
> • He's smart, yet he failed.
> • It's simple, yet effective.
> • She tried hard, yet she didn't succeed.
> 
> Note: Slightly more formal than "but"
> ```
> 
> **NEVERTHELESS / NONETHELESS - Strong formal contrast:**
> 
> ```
> Position: Beginning of sentence or after semicolon
> Use: Very formal writing
> 
> Examples:
> • The plan is risky. Nevertheless, we'll proceed.
> • It's expensive; nevertheless, it's necessary.
> • The evidence is weak. Nonetheless, we must investigate.
> 
> Note: Very formal, academic writing
> ```
> 
> **ON THE OTHER HAND - Alternative viewpoint:**
> 
> ```
> Position: Beginning of sentence
> Use: To present opposite side
> 
> Examples:
> • The job pays well. On the other hand, it's boring.
> • Living in the city is convenient. On the other hand, it's expensive.
> 
> Often used with "on the one hand...":
> • On the one hand, it's cheap. On the other hand, it's low quality.
> ```
> 
> **WHILE / WHEREAS - Comparing differences:**
> 
> ```
> Function: Shows direct comparison or contrast
> Position: Beginning or middle of sentence
> 
> Examples:
> • I like tea, while she likes coffee.
> • Whereas he's outgoing, she's shy.
> • While the North is cold, the South is warm.
> 
> Note: More formal, academic writing
> ```

### 🔄 Cause and Effect Connectors

> [!important]- Showing Reasons and Results
> 
> **BECAUSE - Gives reason:**
> 
> ```
> Function: Introduces cause or reason
> Position: Beginning or middle of sentence
> 
> Examples:
> • I'm happy because it's Friday.
> • Because it was raining, I stayed home.
> • She left early because she was sick.
> 
> Note: Introduces dependent clause
> Cannot stand alone: ✗ "Because it's raining."
> ```
> 
> **BECAUSE OF - Reason with noun phrase:**
> 
> ```
> Function: Similar to "because" but + noun/noun phrase
> Position: Beginning or middle of sentence
> 
> Examples:
> • I'm late because of traffic.
> • Because of the rain, we stayed home.
> • She left because of her illness.
> 
> Compare:
> • because + clause: because it was raining
> • because of + noun: because of the rain
> ```
> 
> **SO - Shows result:**
> 
> ```
> Function: Introduces effect or result
> Position: Between clauses
> 
> Examples:
> • It was raining, so I stayed home.
> • I'm tired, so I'm going to bed.
> • She studied hard, so she passed.
> 
> Note: Informal to neutral register
> Shows cause → effect relationship
> ```
> 
> **THEREFORE - Formal result:**
> 
> ```
> Position: Beginning of sentence or after semicolon
> Use: Formal writing, academic contexts
> 
> Examples:
> • It was raining. Therefore, we stayed home.
> • He failed the test; therefore, he must retake it.
> • The evidence is clear. Therefore, we must act.
> 
> Note: More formal than "so"
> ```
> 
> **CONSEQUENTLY / AS A RESULT - Formal result:**
> 
> ```
> Position: Beginning of sentence
> Use: Formal writing
> 
> Examples:
> • The company lost money. Consequently, it closed.
> • It rained heavily. As a result, the game was cancelled.
> 
> Note: Very formal, academic writing
> ```
> 
> **SINCE / AS - Reason (formal):**
> 
> ```
> Function: Introduces cause (similar to "because")
> Position: Usually beginning of sentence
> 
> Examples:
> • Since it's late, I'll go home.
> • As it was raining, we cancelled the picnic.
> 
> Note: More formal than "because"
> "Since" can also mean time, so context matters
> ```
> 
> **DUE TO - Reason with noun (formal):**
> 
> ```
> Function: Similar to "because of" but more formal
> Position: Beginning or middle of sentence
> 
> Examples:
> • Due to bad weather, the flight was delayed.
> • The delay was due to technical problems.
> • Due to her hard work, she succeeded.
> 
> Note: Formal, often used in announcements
> ```

### 🔢 Sequence Connectors

> [!info]- Showing Order and Time
> 
> **FIRST / FIRSTLY - Starting point:**
> 
> ```
> Position: Beginning of sentence
> Use: Lists, instructions, arguments
> 
> Examples:
> • First, wash your hands.
> • Firstly, I'd like to thank everyone.
> • First, heat the oil.
> 
> Note: "First" is more common in American English
> "Firstly" is more common in British English
> ```
> 
> **SECOND / SECONDLY, THIRD / THIRDLY - Continuing sequence:**
> 
> ```
> Examples:
> • First, add the flour. Second, add the eggs.
> • Firstly, it's expensive. Secondly, it's impractical.
> • First, we'll eat. Then, we'll watch a movie. Third, we'll go home.
> ```
> 
> **THEN - Next step:**
> 
> ```
> Position: Beginning or middle of sentence
> Use: Sequences, instructions, narratives
> 
> Examples:
> • First, boil water. Then, add the pasta.
> • I woke up. Then I had breakfast.
> • We arrived at 8. Then the meeting started.
> 
> Note: Very common in spoken English
> ```
> 
> **NEXT - Following step:**
> 
> ```
> Position: Beginning of sentence
> Use: Instructions, sequences
> 
> Examples:
> • First, preheat the oven. Next, prepare the mixture.
> • We visited the museum. Next, we had lunch.
> 
> Note: Similar to "then" but implies immediate following
> ```
> 
> **AFTER / AFTER THAT - Following action:**
> 
> ```
> After + clause:
> • After I finish work, I'll call you.
> • After we eat, let's go for a walk.
> 
> After that:
> • I went to the store. After that, I went home.
> • We had dinner. After that, we watched TV.
> ```
> 
> **FINALLY / LASTLY - Final point:**
> 
> ```
> Position: Beginning of sentence
> Use: Last item in sequence
> 
> Examples:
> • First, we'll eat. Then, we'll talk. Finally, we'll decide.
> • Finally, I'd like to thank my parents.
> • Lastly, remember to lock the door.
> 
> Note: "Finally" also means "at last" (after waiting)
> • Finally! You're here! (I was waiting)
> ```
> 
> **BEFORE - Earlier action:**
> 
> ```
> Position: Beginning or middle of sentence
> 
> Examples:
> • Before you leave, turn off the lights.
> • Wash your hands before eating.
> • Before we start, any questions?
> ```
> 
> **MEANWHILE / IN THE MEANTIME - During the same time:**
> 
> ```
> Position: Beginning of sentence
> Use: Parallel actions
> 
> Examples:
> • I'll cook dinner. Meanwhile, you set the table.
> • The meeting is at 3. In the meantime, finish your work.
> 
> Note: Shows simultaneous or parallel actions
> ```

### 💡 Example Connectors

> [!note]- Giving Examples and Illustrations
> 
> **FOR EXAMPLE / FOR INSTANCE - Introducing examples:**
> 
> ```
> Position: Beginning of sentence or after noun
> Use: Illustrating a point
> 
> Examples:
> • I like sports. For example, I play tennis and soccer.
> • Many fruits are healthy. For instance, apples are good for you.
> • Some languages, for example Spanish, use gender.
> 
> Note: "For instance" is slightly more formal
> ```
> 
> **SUCH AS - Lists examples:**
> 
> ```
> Position: After noun (introduces list)
> Use: Giving specific examples
> 
> Examples:
> • I like fruits such as apples, oranges, and bananas.
> • Some countries, such as Ecuador and Peru, are in South America.
> • We need supplies such as paper, pens, and notebooks.
> 
> Note: Don't use "like" in formal writing for examples
> ✗ Fruits like apples... (informal)
> ✓ Fruits such as apples... (formal)
> ```
> 
> **LIKE - Informal examples:**
> 
> ```
> Position: After noun
> Use: Casual conversation
> 
> Examples:
> • I like sports like soccer and basketball.
> • Cities like New York are expensive.
> 
> Note: Informal, use "such as" in formal writing
> ```
> 
> **INCLUDING - Adding to a list:**
> 
> ```
> Position: After noun or at end
> 
> Examples:
> • Everyone is coming, including John.
> • We visited many places, including the museum.
> • Many people, including myself, disagree.
> ```

### 🎯 Purpose Connectors

> [!success]- Showing Purpose or Goal
> 
> **TO / IN ORDER TO - Expressing purpose:**
> 
> ```
> Function: Shows the goal or purpose
> Position: Middle or beginning of sentence
> 
> Examples:
> • I study to learn. (informal)
> • I study in order to learn. (formal)
> • To succeed, you must work hard.
> • In order to improve, practice daily.
> 
> Note: "In order to" is more formal than just "to"
> ```
> 
> **SO THAT - Purpose with clause:**
> 
> ```
> Function: Shows intended result
> Position: Middle of sentence
> 
> Examples:
> • I study hard so that I can pass.
> • She saved money so that she could travel.
> • Speak clearly so that everyone understands.
> 
> Note: Introduces a clause (subject + verb)
> ```
> 
> **FOR - Purpose with noun:**
> 
> ```
> Position: Before noun
> 
> Examples:
> • This is for you.
> • I'm studying for the test.
> • We need money for the trip.
> ```

## 📝 Punctuation with Connectors

> [!warning]- Correct Punctuation Rules
> 
> **Coordinating conjunctions (FANBOYS: For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So):**
> 
> ```
> Between two independent clauses:
> , + connector
> 
> ✓ I like tea, and she likes coffee.
> ✓ It's raining, but we'll go out.
> ✓ She studied hard, so she passed.
> 
> Between words/phrases (no comma needed):
> ✓ I like tea and coffee. (no comma)
> ✓ He's smart but lazy. (no comma)
> ```
> 
> **Subordinating conjunctions (because, although, if, when, etc.):**
> 
> ```
> Beginning of sentence:
> Connector + clause, + main clause
> 
> ✓ Because it was raining, we stayed home.
> ✓ Although she's tired, she's working.
> 
> Middle of sentence (no comma):
> Main clause + connector + clause
> 
> ✓ We stayed home because it was raining.
> ✓ She's working although she's tired.
> ```
> 
> **Transitional phrases (however, therefore, moreover, etc.):**
> 
> ```
> Beginning of sentence:
> Connector, + sentence
> 
> ✓ However, I disagree.
> ✓ Therefore, we must act.
> 
> After semicolon:
> ; + connector, + sentence
> 
> ✓ I like tea; however, I don't like coffee.
> ✓ It rained; therefore, we stayed home.
> 
> Middle of sentence:
> , connector,
> 
> ✓ The plan, however, failed.
> ✓ We must, therefore, try again.
> ```

## 💡 Common Mistakes and Tips

> [!warning]- Errores Comunes para Hispanohablantes
> 
> **Error 1: Confundir "because" y "because of"**
> 
> ```
> BECAUSE + clause (subject + verb):
> ✓ I'm late because the bus was late.
> ✗ I'm late because the traffic.
> 
> BECAUSE OF + noun/noun phrase:
> ✓ I'm late because of traffic.
> ✗ I'm late because of the bus was late.
> 
> Regla: because = verbo / because of = sustantivo
> ```
> 
> **Error 2: Usar "so" al inicio de oración (muy informal)**
> 
> ```
> En español: "Así que..." es aceptable
> En inglés formal: evitar "So" al inicio
> 
> Informal (hablado):
> ✓ So, what do you think?
> 
> Formal (escrito):
> ✗ So, the plan failed.
> ✓ Therefore, the plan failed.
> 
> Mid-sentence está bien:
> ✓ The plan failed, so we tried again.
> ```
> 
> **Error 3: Puntuation incorrecta con "however"**
> 
> ```
> ✗ I like tea, however I don't like coffee. (comma splice!)
> ✓ I like tea. However, I don't like coffee.
> ✓ I like tea; however, I don't like coffee.
> 
> "However" NO es como "but" - necesita punto o punto y coma antes
> ```
> 
> **Error 4: Usar "although" y "but" juntos**
> 
> ```
> ✗ Although it was raining, but we went out.
> ✓ Although it was raining, we went out.
> ✓ It was raining, but we went out.
> 
> Usa UNO u OTRO, no ambos
> ```
> 
> **Error 5: Confundir "like" y "such as" en escritura formal**
> 
> ```
> Informal/hablado:
> ✓ I like sports like soccer and tennis.
> 
> Formal/escrito:
> ✗ I like sports like soccer and tennis.
> ✓ I like sports such as soccer and tennis.
> 
> "Like" = informal
> "Such as" = formal
> ```
> 
> **Error 6: Omitir comas necesarias**
> 
> ```
> ✗ Because it was raining we stayed home.
> ✓ Because it was raining, we stayed home.
> 
> ✗ First heat the oil. Then add the onions.
> ✓ First, heat the oil. Then, add the onions.
> 
> Regla: connector al inicio → coma después
> ```
> 
> **Error 7: Traducir literalmente del español**
> 
> ```
> "Entonces" no siempre = "then"
> 
> Español: "Entonces, ¿qué piensas?"
> ✗ Then, what do you think?
> ✓ So, what do you think?
> 
> "Then" = tiempo/secuencia
> "So" = resultado o pregunta conversacional
> ```

> [!tip]- Consejos y Trucos de Memoria
> 
> **Tip 1: FANBOYS para coordinación**
> 
> ```
> Recuerda: FANBOYS (coordinadores básicos)
> 
> F = For (porque - formal)
> A = And (y)
> N = Nor (ni)
> B = But (pero)
> O = Or (o)
> Y = Yet (sin embargo)
> S = So (entonces, así que)
> 
> Estos conectan oraciones independientes con coma antes:
> I like tea, and she likes coffee.
> ```
> 
> **Tip 2: Niveles de formalidad**
> 
> ```
> INFORMAL (hablado):
> so, plus, too, like
> 
> NEUTRAL (general):
> and, but, because, also, then
> 
> FORMAL (escrito/académico):
> however, therefore, moreover, furthermore, such as
> 
> Ejemplo del mismo concepto:
> - Plus, it's expensive. (muy informal)
> - And it's expensive. (neutral)
> - Moreover, it's expensive. (formal)
> ```
> 
> **Tip 3: Cláusula vs. frase**
> 
> ```
> CLÁUSULA = sujeto + verbo
> FRASE = solo sustantivo/frase
> 
> BECAUSE + cláusula:
> • because it was raining (it + was = sujeto + verbo)
> 
> BECAUSE OF + frase:
> • because of the rain (solo sustantivo)
> 
> Mismo patrón:
> - although + cláusula / despite + frase
> - so that + cláusula / for + frase
> ```
> 
> **Tip 4: Posición de "also"**
> 
> ```
> Regla fácil para "also":
> 
> DESPUÉS de BE:
> • She is also smart.
> 
> ANTES de otros verbos:
> • She also speaks French.
> 
> NUNCA al final:
> ✗ She speaks French also.
> ✓ She speaks French, too. (usa "too" al final)
> ```
> 
> **Tip 5: Para recordar orden de secuencia**
> 
> ```
> Piensa en una receta:
> 
> First (primero)
> ↓
> Then/Next (luego/siguiente)
> ↓
> After that (después de eso)
> ↓
> Finally (finalmente)
> 
> Ejemplo:
> First, wash your hands.
> Then, cut the vegetables.
> After that, heat the oil.
> Finally, cook the meal.
> ```
> 
> **Tip 6: "However" vs "But" - posición**
> 
> ```
> BUT = siempre ENTRE dos oraciones (mismo enunciado):
> • I like tea, but I don't
> ```
