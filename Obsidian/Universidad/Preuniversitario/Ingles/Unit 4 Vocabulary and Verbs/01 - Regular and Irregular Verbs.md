# 🎯 Regular and Irregular Verbs

## 🌟 What Are Regular and Irregular Verbs?

> [!info] Definition **Verbs are action or state words that form the core of every sentence. In English, verbs are classified as REGULAR or IRREGULAR based on how they form their past tense and past participle.**
> 
> **Key points:**
> 
> - **Regular verbs** follow a predictable pattern: add **-ed** to form past tense and past participle
> - **Irregular verbs** do NOT follow this pattern and must be memorized
> - There are approximately **200 common irregular verbs** in English
> - Understanding verb forms is essential for proper tense usage
> - The three principal parts: **Base Form → Past Simple → Past Participle**

### 📖 Historical Context

> [!note]- Development of English Verb Forms
> 
> **Old English period (450-1100 AD):**
> 
> - **Germanic roots:** English inherited strong (irregular) verbs from Germanic languages
> - **Strong verbs:** Changed internal vowels (sing-sang-sung pattern)
> - **Weak verbs:** Added dental suffixes (-ed pattern)
> - Most verbs were originally irregular
> 
> **Middle English period (1100-1500 AD):**
> 
> - **Norman French influence:** Many new verbs borrowed from French
> - **Regularization begins:** Some irregular verbs became regular
> - **-ed suffix standardizes:** Pattern becomes dominant for new verbs
> 
> **Early Modern English (1500-1700 AD):**
> 
> - **Shakespeare's era:** Many irregular forms still in flux
> - **Printing press:** Standardization of spelling and forms
> - Some verbs had multiple accepted forms
> 
> **Modern English (1700-present):**
> 
> - **Continuing simplification:** More irregular verbs becoming regular
> - **New verbs default to regular:** "google" → "googled", "text" → "texted"
> - **Regional variations:** Some differences between British/American English
> - **Irregular verbs persist:** Core irregular verbs remain stable

## 📘 Regular Verbs

### ✅ What Are Regular Verbs?

> [!success]- Complete Guide to Regular Verbs
> 
> **Definition:** Regular verbs form their past simple and past participle by adding **-ed** (or **-d** if the verb already ends in 'e') to the base form.
> 
> **The three principal parts:**
> 
> ```
> Base Form → Past Simple → Past Participle
> 
> walk → walked → walked
> play → played → played
> study → studied → studied
> ```
> 
> **Why they're called "regular":**
> 
> ```
> They follow a consistent, predictable pattern.
> If you know the base form, you can create the past forms automatically.
> ```
> 
> **Percentage of English verbs:**
> 
> ```
> • Regular verbs: ~90% of all English verbs
> • New verbs: Almost always regular
> • Irregular verbs: ~10% but include the most common verbs
> ```

### 🔤 Spelling Rules for Regular Verbs

> [!tip]- Rules for Adding -ed
> 
> **Rule 1: Standard verbs → add -ed**
> 
> ```
> work → worked
> clean → cleaned
> answer → answered
> listen → listened
> ```
> 
> **Rule 2: Verbs ending in -e → add -d only**
> 
> ```
> live → lived
> love → loved
> dance → danced
> arrive → arrived
> believe → believed
> ```
> 
> **Rule 3: Verbs ending in consonant + y → change y to i, add -ed**
> 
> ```
> study → studied
> cry → cried
> try → tried
> carry → carried
> worry → worried
> 
> BUT: If vowel + y, just add -ed:
> play → played
> enjoy → enjoyed
> stay → stayed
> ```
> 
> **Rule 4: One-syllable verbs ending in consonant-vowel-consonant → double final consonant + -ed**
> 
> ```
> stop → stopped
> plan → planned
> drop → dropped
> rob → robbed
> 
> Exception: Do NOT double w, x, y:
> snow → snowed (not snowed)
> fix → fixed (not fixxed)
> play → played (not playyed)
> ```
> 
> **Rule 5: Two-syllable verbs with stress on SECOND syllable → double final consonant**
> 
> ```
> prefer → preferred (stress on -FER)
> admit → admitted (stress on -MIT)
> occur → occurred (stress on -CUR)
> 
> BUT: If stress on FIRST syllable, don't double:
> visit → visited (stress on VIS-)
> open → opened (stress on O-)
> ```

### 🔊 Pronunciation of -ed Endings

> [!important]- Three Ways to Pronounce -ed
> 
> **Sound 1: /t/ (voiceless T sound)**
> 
> ```
> When the base verb ends in voiceless sounds: /p/, /k/, /f/, /s/, /ʃ/, /tʃ/
> 
> Examples:
> • walked /wɔːkt/
> • stopped /stɒpt/
> • laughed /læft/
> • kissed /kɪst/
> • washed /wɒʃt/
> • watched /wɒtʃt/
> 
> Memory tip: These sounds are made without vibrating your vocal cords
> ```
> 
> **Sound 2: /d/ (voiced D sound)**
> 
> ```
> When the base verb ends in voiced sounds: /b/, /g/, /v/, /z/, /ʒ/, /dʒ/, /m/, /n/, /ŋ/, /l/, /r/, or any vowel
> 
> Examples:
> • played /pleɪd/
> • robbed /rɒbd/
> • lived /lɪvd/
> • cleaned /kliːnd/
> • called /kɔːld/
> • occurred /əˈkɜːd/
> 
> Memory tip: These sounds make your vocal cords vibrate
> ```
> 
> **Sound 3: /ɪd/ or /əd/ (extra syllable - ID sound)**
> 
> ```
> When the base verb ends in /t/ or /d/ sounds
> 
> Examples:
> • wanted /ˈwɒn.tɪd/ (2 syllables)
> • needed /ˈniː.dɪd/ (2 syllables)
> • decided /dɪˈsaɪ.dɪd/ (3 syllables)
> • started /ˈstɑː.tɪd/ (2 syllables)
> • ended /ˈen.dɪd/ (2 syllables)
> 
> Memory tip: Adds an extra syllable to the word
> ```
> 
> **Practice test:**
> 
> ```
> Identify the -ed sound:
> 
> 1. cooked → /t/
> 2. smiled → /d/
> 3. waited → /ɪd/
> 4. arrived → /d/
> 5. helped → /t/
> 6. added → /ɪd/
> ```

## 🔄 Irregular Verbs

### ❌ What Are Irregular Verbs?

> [!warning]- Complete Guide to Irregular Verbs
> 
> **Definition:** Irregular verbs do NOT form their past simple and past participle by adding -ed. Instead, they change in unpredictable ways that must be memorized.
> 
> **Why they exist:**
> 
> ```
> Historical reasons:
> • These verbs come from Old English "strong verbs"
> • They changed internal vowels instead of adding endings
> • They're the oldest, most commonly used verbs in English
> • Native speakers learned them as children through frequent exposure
> ```
> 
> **Patterns of irregular verbs:**
> 
> ```
> Type 1: All three forms are THE SAME
> Base → Past → Past Participle
> • cut → cut → cut
> • put → put → put
> • cost → cost → cost
> 
> Type 2: Past and Past Participle are THE SAME
> • buy → bought → bought
> • bring → brought → brought
> • teach → taught → taught
> 
> Type 3: All three forms are DIFFERENT
> • go → went → gone
> • see → saw → seen
> • do → did → done
> 
> Type 4: Base and Past Participle are THE SAME
> • come → came → come
> • run → ran → run
> • become → became → become
> ```
> 
> **Why irregular verbs are important:**
> 
> ```
> • They include the MOST COMMON verbs in English
> • be, have, do, go, get, make, see, know, say, think
> • You cannot communicate effectively without knowing them
> • Native speakers use them constantly in everyday speech
> ```

### 💡 Memory Techniques for Irregular Verbs

> [!tip]- Strategies to Remember Irregular Verbs
> 
> **Technique 1: Group by similar patterns**
> 
> ```
> Learn verbs with similar changes together:
> 
> -ing verbs:
> sing → sang → sung
> ring → rang → rung
> spring → sprang → sprung
> 
> -ow verbs:
> blow → blew → blown
> grow → grew → grown
> know → knew → known
> throw → threw → thrown
> ```
> 
> **Technique 2: Create stories or sentences**
> 
> ```
> Use multiple irregular verbs in one memorable story:
> 
> "I went to the store and bought some bread. 
>  I ate it when I came home, then I slept well."
> 
> (went, bought, ate, came, slept - all irregular)
> ```
> 
> **Technique 3: Focus on high-frequency verbs first**
> 
> ```
> Master the most common ones before moving to rare verbs:
> Priority: be, have, do, go, get, make, see, know
> ```
> 
> **Technique 4: Practice with flashcards**
> 
> ```
> Front: Base form + Spanish translation
> Back: Past simple + Past participle
> 
> Review daily, focusing on verbs you struggle with.
> ```
> 
> **Technique 5: Use in context**
> 
> ```
> Write personal sentences about your life:
> 
> "Yesterday I went to the beach."
> "Last year I saw my favorite band."

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes with Irregular Verbs

> [!warning]- Errors to Avoid
> 
> **Mistake 1: Adding -ed to irregular verbs**
> 
> ```
> ✗ I goed to the store. → ✓ I went to the store.
> ✗ She eated dinner. → ✓ She ate dinner.
> ✗ They breaked the window. → ✓ They broke the window.
> ✗ He thinked about it. → ✓ He thought about it.
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 2: Confusing past simple and past participle**
> 
> ```
> ✗ I have went there. → ✓ I have gone there.
> ✗ She has ate lunch. → ✓ She has eaten lunch.
> ✗ They have saw the movie. → ✓ They have seen the movie.
> ✗ He has wrote a letter. → ✓ He has written a letter.
> 
> Remember: Use past participle after have/has/had!
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 3: Using wrong form entirely**
> 
> ```
> ✗ I seen him yesterday. → ✓ I saw him yesterday.
> ✗ She done her homework. → ✓ She did her homework.
> ✗ They come here last week. → ✓ They came here last week.
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 4: Confusing similar irregular verbs**
> 
> ```
> lie (yacer) → lay → lain
> lay (poner) → laid → laid
> 
> ✗ I lied down on the bed. → ✓ I lay down on the bed.
> ✗ She laid on the couch. → ✓ She lay on the couch.
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 5: Treating irregular verbs as regular**
> 
> ```
> ✗ The sun rised. → ✓ The sun rose.
> ✗ He feeled sick. → ✓ He felt sick.
> ✗ We buyed a car. → ✓ We bought a car.
> ```

> [!warning]- Commonly Confused Irregular Verb Pairs
> 
> **1. LIE vs LAY**
> 
> ```
> lie (yacer/acostarse) - intransitive (no object):
> • Present: lie → Past: lay → Past Participle: lain
> • I lie down on my bed every night.
> • Yesterday I lay down for an hour.
> • I have lain here for too long.
> 
> lay (poner/colocar) - transitive (needs object):
> • Present: lay → Past: laid → Past Participle: laid
> • Please lay the book on the table.
> • She laid the baby in the crib.
> • They have laid the foundation.
> ```
> 
> **2. RISE vs RAISE**
> 
> ```
> rise (levantarse/subir) - intransitive (no object):
> • Present: rise → Past: rose → Past Participle: risen
> • The sun rises every morning.
> • The sun rose at 6 AM today.
> • Prices have risen significantly.
> 
> raise (levantar/aumentar) - transitive (needs object):
> • Present: raise → Past: raised → Past Participle: raised (REGULAR!)
> • Please raise your hand.
> • She raised the flag.
> • They have raised $10,000.
> ```
> 
> **3. SIT vs SET**
> 
> ```
> sit (sentarse) - intransitive (no object):
> • Present: sit → Past: sat → Past Participle: sat
> • I sit here every day.
> • She sat on the chair.
> • We have sat here for hours.
> 
> set (poner/establecer) - transitive (needs object):
> • Present: set → Past: set → Past Participle: set
> • Set the book on the table.
> • He set the alarm for 7 AM.
> • They have set a new record.
> ```
> 
> **4. HANG (two different verbs!)**
> 
> ```
> hang (colgar objetos) - irregular:
> • Present: hang → Past: hung → Past Participle: hung
> • Hang the picture on the wall.
> • She hung the picture yesterday.
> • The picture has hung there for years.
> 
> hang (ejecutar por ahorcamiento) - regular:
> • Present: hang → Past: hanged → Past Participle: hanged
> • They hanged the criminal. (historical/legal context)
> ```

## 📝 Advanced Concepts

> [!note]- Irregular Verbs in Different Tenses
> 
> **Present Perfect vs Past Simple:**
> 
> ```
> Past Simple (specific time in the past):
> • I saw that movie last week.
> • She went to Paris in 2020.
> • They ate dinner at 7 PM.
> 
> Present Perfect (indefinite past or continuing relevance):
> • I have seen that movie before. (don't know exactly when)
> • She has gone to Paris many times. (life experience)
> • They have eaten dinner already. (recent past, relevant now)
> ```
> 
> **Past Perfect (earlier past action):**
> 
> ```
> • I had already eaten when she arrived.
>   (eating happened before arriving)
> 
> • They had gone home before the rain started.
>   (going happened before raining)
> 
> • She had written the email before the meeting.
>   (writing happened before meeting)
> ```
> 
> **Future Perfect (will be completed by future time):**
> 
> ```
> • By tomorrow, I will have finished the project.
> • By next year, she will have written her book.
> • By the time you arrive, we will have eaten.
> ```

> [!info]- Irregular Verbs in Passive Voice
> 
> **Formation: be + past participle**
> 
> ```
> Active: Someone wrote this book in 1950.
> Passive: This book was written in 1950.
> 
> Active: They have eaten all the pizza.
> Passive: All the pizza has been eaten.
> 
> Active: She will give you the keys.
> Passive: The keys will be given to you.
> 
> Active: People speak English worldwide.
> Passive: English is spoken worldwide.
> ```
> 
> **Common passive constructions:**
> 
> ```
> • The window was broken by the ball.
>   (break → broke → broken)
> 
> • The letter has been written.
>   (write → wrote → written)
> 
> • The car is being driven carefully.
>   (drive → drove → driven)
> 
> • The truth must be told.
>   (tell → told → told)
> ```
