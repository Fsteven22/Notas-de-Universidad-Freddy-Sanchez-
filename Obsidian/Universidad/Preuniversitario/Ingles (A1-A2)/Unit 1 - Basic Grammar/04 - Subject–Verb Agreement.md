# 🎯 Subject–Verb Agreement

## 🌟 What Is Subject–Verb Agreement?

> [!info] Definition **Subject–verb agreement means that the subject and verb in a sentence must match in number. If the subject is singular, the verb must be singular. If the subject is plural, the verb must be plural. This grammatical rule ensures clarity and correctness in English sentences.**
> 
> **Key points:**
> 
> - **Subject:** The person, thing, or idea performing the action
> - **Verb:** The action or state of being
> - **Agreement:** Subject and verb must "agree" in number (singular or plural)
> - **Present tense:** Most affected by agreement rules
> - **Third person singular:** Gets special treatment (adds -s/-es)
> - **Collective nouns, compound subjects:** Special agreement rules apply
> - Mastering agreement is essential for grammatically correct English

### 📖 Historical Context

> [!note]- Development of Subject–Verb Agreement
> 
> **Old English period (450-1100 AD):**
> 
> - **Complex verb conjugation:** Different endings for each person and number
> - **Example - "be" verb:**
>     
>     ```
>     I am (eom)          We are (sind/sindon)You are (eart)      You are (sind/sindon)He/She/It is (is)   They are (sind/sindon)
>     ```
>     
> - **Regular verbs:** Had distinct endings (-e, -est, -eþ, -aþ)
> - **Clear person/number markers:** Each form was different
> - **Gender agreement:** Adjectives and pronouns agreed with noun gender
> 
> **Middle English period (1100-1500):**
> 
> - **Simplification begins:** Many endings lost or merged
> - **-eth becomes standard:** Third person singular (he loveth)
> - **Plural forms merge:** Different plural endings become one (-en, later -e)
> - **Loss of thou/thee distinction begins:** "You" spreads to singular use
> - **Northern dialects:** Start using -s instead of -eth
> 
> **Early Modern English (1500-1700):**
> 
> - **-s replaces -eth:** "He loves" instead of "He loveth"
> - **Shakespeare's transitional usage:** Both forms appear
>     - "He loveth" (older, formal)
>     - "He loves" (newer, becoming standard)
> - **Modern pattern emerges:** Only third person singular gets -s
> - **Other persons lose endings:** "I love, you love, we love, they love"
> 
> **Modern English (1700-present):**
> 
> - **Current system established:** Simple pattern
>     
>     ```
>     I/you/we/they + base verbHe/she/it + base verb + s/es
>     ```
>     
> - **Only one inflection remains:** Third person singular -s/-es
> - **"Be" remains irregular:** Most complex verb (am/is/are/was/were)
> - **Result:** One of the simplest agreement systems among European languages
> - **But:** Still causes errors for learners because of exceptions

## 📐 Basic Agreement Rules

### 🔵 The Fundamental Rule

> [!success]- Singular and Plural Agreement
> 
> **The core principle:**
> 
> ```
> SINGULAR SUBJECT → SINGULAR VERB (add -s/-es)
> PLURAL SUBJECT → PLURAL VERB (no -s)
> 
> This seems backwards from Spanish!
> In Spanish: plural subject → plural verb (-n)
> In English: plural subject → base verb (no -s)
> ```
> 
> **Present simple - affirmative:**
> 
> ```
> Singular (third person):
> • I work           • He works
> • You work         • She works
> • We work          • It works
> • They work
> 
> Notice: Only HE/SHE/IT adds -s
> All others use base form
> ```
> 
> **Examples with regular verbs:**
> 
> ```
> WORK:
> ✓ The student works hard.      (singular → add -s)
> ✓ The students work hard.      (plural → no -s)
> 
> PLAY:
> ✓ She plays tennis.            (singular → add -s)
> ✓ They play tennis.            (plural → no -s)
> 
> LIVE:
> ✓ My brother lives in Quito.   (singular → add -s)
> ✓ My brothers live in Quito.   (plural → no -s)
> 
> EAT:
> ✓ The dog eats meat.           (singular → add -s)
> ✓ The dogs eat meat.           (plural → no -s)
> ```
> 
> **Adding -s/-es to verbs:**
> 
> ```
> Most verbs: add -s
> • work → works
> • play → plays
> • eat → eats
> • run → runs
> 
> Verbs ending in -s, -x, -z, -ch, -sh: add -es
> • pass → passes
> • fix → fixes
> • buzz → buzzes
> • catch → catches
> • wash → washes
> 
> Verbs ending in consonant + y: change to -ies
> • study → studies
> • try → tries
> • fly → flies
> • cry → cries
> 
> Verbs ending in vowel + y: just add -s
> • play → plays
> • say → says
> • buy → buys
> • enjoy → enjoys
> 
> Have: irregular
> • have → has
> ```

### 🔴 The Verb "TO BE"

> [!important]- Complete Conjugation of "BE"
> 
> **Present tense:**
> 
> ```
> Singular:                Plural:
> I am                     We are
> You are                  You are
> He/She/It is            They are
> 
> Examples:
> ✓ I am a student.
> ✓ You are smart.
> ✓ He is tall.
> ✓ She is happy.
> ✓ It is cold.
> ✓ We are friends.
> ✓ They are busy.
> ```
> 
> **Past tense:**
> 
> ```
> Singular:                Plural:
> I was                    We were
> You were                 You were
> He/She/It was           They were
> 
> Examples:
> ✓ I was tired yesterday.
> ✓ You were right.
> ✓ He was sick.
> ✓ She was at home.
> ✓ It was sunny.
> ✓ We were late.
> ✓ They were happy.
> ```
> 
> **Common errors with "BE":**
> 
> ```
> ✗ I is → ✓ I am
> ✗ You is → ✓ You are
> ✗ He are → ✓ He is
> ✗ We is → ✓ We are
> ✗ They is → ✓ They are
> 
> ✗ I were → ✓ I was (past)
> ✗ He were → ✓ He was (past)
> ✗ They was → ✓ They were (past)
> ```
> 
> **Contractions:**
> 
> ```
> Present:
> I'm = I am               We're = We are
> You're = You are         You're = You are
> He's = He is            They're = They are
> She's = She is
> It's = It is
> 
> Past (negative only):
> wasn't = was not
> weren't = were not
> ```

### 🟡 Modal Verbs and Auxiliaries

> [!info]- Agreement with Helper Verbs
> 
> **Modal verbs (NEVER change form):**
> 
> ```
> can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would
> 
> ✓ I can swim.
> ✓ He can swim.         (NOT "cans")
> ✓ She must go.         (NOT "musts")
> ✓ They will arrive.    (NOT "wills")
> 
> Modals ALWAYS use base form of main verb:
> ✓ She can play piano.  (NOT "plays")
> ✓ He must study.       (NOT "studies")
> ```
> 
> **DO/DOES (present simple questions and negatives):**
> 
> ```
> Questions:
> • Do I/you/we/they work?
> • Does he/she/it work?
> 
> Negatives:
> • I/you/we/they do not (don't) work
> • He/she/it does not (doesn't) work
> 
> Important: Main verb stays in base form!
> ✓ Does she work? (NOT "Does she works?")
> ✓ He doesn't like it. (NOT "doesn't likes")
> ```
> 
> **HAVE/HAS (present perfect):**
> 
> ```
> • I/you/we/they have worked
> • He/she/it has worked
> 
> Examples:
> ✓ I have finished.
> ✓ She has finished.
> ✓ They have finished.
> ```

## 🎯 Complex Agreement Situations

### 📝 Compound Subjects

> [!warning]- Special Rules for Multiple Subjects
> 
> **Rule 1: AND = Plural verb**
> 
> ```
> When subjects are connected by "and," use plural verb:
> 
> ✓ Tom and Mary are students.
> ✓ The book and the pen are on the table.
> ✓ My brother and I work together.
> ✓ Coffee and tea are my favorite drinks.
> 
> Exception - when subjects form a single unit:
> ✓ Bread and butter is my favorite snack.
> ✓ Peanut butter and jelly is delicious.
> ✓ Bacon and eggs is a typical breakfast.
> ```
> 
> **Rule 2: OR/NOR = Verb agrees with nearest subject**
> 
> ```
> The verb agrees with the subject closest to it:
> 
> ✓ Tom or Mary is coming.      (Mary = singular → is)
> ✓ Tom or his friends are coming. (friends = plural → are)
> ✓ His friends or Tom is coming. (Tom = singular → is)
> 
> ✓ Neither the teacher nor the students are ready.
> ✓ Neither the students nor the teacher is ready.
> 
> ✓ Either John or his brothers are responsible.
> ✓ Either his brothers or John is responsible.
> ```
> 
> **Rule 3: As well as, along with, together with**
> 
> ```
> The verb agrees with the FIRST subject only:
> 
> ✓ The teacher, as well as the students, is ready.
>   (teacher = main subject → is)
> 
> ✓ The students, as well as the teacher, are ready.
>   (students = main subject → are)
> 
> ✓ John, along with his friends, is coming.
> ✓ My parents, together with my sister, are arriving tomorrow.
> 
> Think: The phrase in the middle is extra information, not part of subject
> ```

### 🔢 Indefinite Pronouns

> [!important]- Agreement with Indefinite Pronouns
> 
> **Always SINGULAR (use IS/WAS/VERB+S):**
> 
> ```
> each, every, either, neither, one, no one, nobody, nothing,
> someone, somebody, something, anyone, anybody, anything,
> everyone, everybody, everything
> 
> ✓ Everyone is here.           (NOT "are")
> ✓ Somebody has my pen.        (NOT "have")
> ✓ Each student has a book.    (NOT "have")
> ✓ Every child needs love.     (NOT "need")
> ✓ Neither option is good.     (NOT "are")
> ✓ Nobody knows the answer.    (NOT "know")
> ✓ Something smells good.      (NOT "smell")
> 
> Even with "of + plural noun":
> ✓ Each of the students has a book.    (NOT "have")
> ✓ One of the girls is my sister.     (NOT "are")
> ✓ Every one of them is guilty.       (NOT "are")
> ```
> 
> **Always PLURAL (use ARE/WERE/BASE VERB):**
> 
> ```
> both, few, many, several, others
> 
> ✓ Both are correct.
> ✓ Few understand this.
> ✓ Many have tried.
> ✓ Several are missing.
> ✓ Others are coming later.
> 
> ✓ Both of the answers are correct.
> ✓ Many of the students study hard.
> ```
> 
> **Singular OR Plural (depends on noun after "of"):**
> 
> ```
> all, any, most, none, some
> 
> With uncountable noun → SINGULAR:
> ✓ All of the water is gone.
> ✓ Some of the money is missing.
> ✓ Most of the work is done.
> 
> With countable plural noun → PLURAL:
> ✓ All of the students are here.
> ✓ Some of the books are old.
> ✓ Most of the people are happy.
> ✓ None of the answers are correct. (or "is" - both acceptable)
> ```

### 👥 Collective Nouns

> [!note]- Groups as Singular or Plural
> 
> **Common collective nouns:**
> 
> ```
> team, family, class, committee, group, crowd, audience,
> jury, staff, government, company, band, crew
> ```
> 
> **American English: Usually SINGULAR**
> 
> ```
> Treating the group as a single unit:
> 
> ✓ The team is winning.
> ✓ My family is large.
> ✓ The class is difficult.
> ✓ The committee has decided.
> ✓ The government is working on it.
> ✓ The company is hiring.
> ```
> 
> **British English: Often PLURAL**
> 
> ```
> Emphasizing individual members:
> 
> ✓ The team are arguing among themselves. (UK)
> ✓ The family are all coming to dinner. (UK)
> ✓ The government are divided on this issue. (UK)
> ```
> 
> **In American English, use plural when emphasizing individuals:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ The team are showering and changing. (individual actions)
> ✓ The family are each choosing their own dessert.
> ✓ The jury were divided in their opinions.
> ```
> 
> **Always plural:**
> 
> ```
> police, people, cattle
> 
> ✓ The police are investigating.  (NOT "is")
> ✓ People are waiting.             (NOT "is")
> ✓ The cattle are grazing.         (NOT "is")
> ```

### 📊 Special Subjects

> [!warning]- Tricky Agreement Situations
> 
> **1. Subjects with prepositional phrases:**
> 
> ```
> The verb agrees with the MAIN subject, not the object of preposition:
> 
> ✓ The book on the tables is mine.
>   (book = subject → singular → is)
> 
> ✓ The books on the table are mine.
>   (books = subject → plural → are)
> 
> ✓ One of the students is absent.
>   (one = subject → singular → is)
> 
> ✓ The students in the class are smart.
>   (students = subject → plural → are)
> 
> Common prepositions: of, on, in, at, with, for, to, from
> ```
> 
> **2. There is/are constructions:**
> 
> ```
> The verb agrees with what comes AFTER "there":
> 
> ✓ There is a book on the table.      (a book = singular)
> ✓ There are books on the table.      (books = plural)
> ✓ There is water in the glass.       (water = uncountable)
> ✓ There are five students here.      (five students = plural)
> 
> ✗ There is many people.  → ✓ There are many people.
> ✗ There are a problem.   → ✓ There is a problem.
> ```
> 
> **3. Inverted sentences (verb before subject):**
> 
> ```
> In questions and inverted sentences, find the real subject:
> 
> ✓ Here comes the bus.        (the bus = singular)
> ✓ Here come the buses.       (the buses = plural)
> 
> ✓ On the table is a book.    (a book = singular)
> ✓ On the table are books.    (books = plural)
> ```
> 
> **4. Titles, names, and words as words:**
> 
> ```
> Titles and names are ALWAYS singular:
> 
> ✓ "The Chronicles of Narnia" is a great book.
> ✓ The United States is a large country.
> ✓ The Beatles was a famous band. (the band itself)
> ✓ "Friends" was a popular TV show.
> 
> Words referred to as words are singular:
> ✓ "Scissors" is a plural noun.
> ✓ "Children" is the plural of "child."
> ```
> 
> **5. Amounts and measurements:**
> 
> ```
> Expressions of time, money, distance, weight = SINGULAR:
> 
> ✓ Five dollars is too much.
> ✓ Ten kilometers is a long distance.
> ✓ Three hours is enough time.
> ✓ Two kilos is perfect.
> 
> BUT if referring to individual items = PLURAL:
> ✓ Five coins are on the table. (individual coins)
> ```
> 
> **6. Fractions and percentages:**
> 
> ```
> Agree with the noun in the "of" phrase:
> 
> ✓ Half of the cake is gone.          (cake = singular)
> ✓ Half of the students are here.     (students = plural)
> 
> ✓ 50% of the work is done.           (work = singular)
> ✓ 50% of the workers are on break.   (workers = plural)
> ```

## 💡 Common Mistakes and Tips

> [!warning]- Errores Comunes para Hispanohablantes
> 
> **Error 1: Confundir el patrón español con el inglés**
> 
> ```
> En español: sujeto plural → verbo plural (-n)
> • Los estudiantes estudian
> 
> En inglés: sujeto plural → verbo SIN -s
> • The students study (NOT "studies")
> 
> ✗ The students studies → ✓ The students study
> ✗ They works → ✓ They work
> ✗ My friends likes → ✓ My friends like
> ```
> 
> **Error 2: Olvidar la -s con he/she/it**
> 
> ```
> ✗ She work every day. → ✓ She works every day.
> ✗ He like coffee. → ✓ He likes coffee.
> ✗ It cost too much. → ✓ It costs too much.
> ✗ My brother live in Quito. → ✓ My brother lives in Quito.
> ```
> 
> **Error 3: Everyone/Everybody con verbo plural**
> 
> ```
> ✗ Everyone are here. → ✓ Everyone is here.
> ✗ Everybody have finished. → ✓ Everybody has finished.
> ✗ Each of them are ready. → ✓ Each of them is ready.
> 
> Recuerda: -one, -body, -thing = siempre SINGULAR
> ```
> 
> **Error 4: There is/are incorrecto**
> 
> ```
> ✗ There is many people. → ✓ There are many people.
> ✗ There are a problem. → ✓ There is a problem.
> ✗ There is three books. → ✓ There are three books.
> 
> Truco: Mira lo que viene DESPUÉS de there is/are
> ```
> 
> **Error 5: Ignorar frases preposicionales**
> 
> ```
> ✗ One of the students are absent. → ✓ One of the students is absent.
>    (ONE = sujeto, no "students")
> 
> ✗ The book on the tables are mine. → ✓ The book on the tables is mine.
>    (BOOK = sujeto, no "tables")
> 
> Truco: Tacha la frase preposicional y encuentra el sujeto real
> ```
> 
> **Error 6: Do/Does con verbo conjugado**
> 
> ```
> ✗ Does she works? → ✓ Does she work?
> ✗ Do they works? → ✓ Do they work?
> ✗ He doesn't likes it. → ✓ He doesn't like it.
> 
> Regla: DO/DOES lleva la conjugación, verbo principal queda en base
> ```
> 
> **Error 7: Police, people como singular**
> 
> ```
> ✗ The police is coming. → ✓ The police are coming.
> ✗ People is waiting. → ✓ People are waiting.
> ✗ The cattle is in the field. → ✓ The cattle are in the field.
> 
> Estas palabras son SIEMPRE plurales en inglés
> ```

> [!tip]- Consejos y Trucos de Memoria
> 
> **Tip 1: La regla "al revés"**
> 
> ```
> Piensa: En inglés es AL REVÉS del español
> 
> Español:
> • Yo trabajo (sin terminación especial)
> • Él trabaja (sin terminación especial)
> • Ellos trabajan (+ n para plural)
> 
> Inglés:
> • I work (sin -s)
> • He works (+ s para singular!)
> • They work (sin -s para plural)
> 
> Solo HE/SHE/IT es diferente (add -s)
> ```
> 
> **Tip 2: Para memorizar indefinite pronouns**
> 
> ```
> Palabras terminadas en -ONE, -BODY, -THING = SINGULAR
> 
> SomeONE is → SomeBODY is → SomeTHING is
> EveryONE is → EveryBODY is → EveryTHING is
> AnyONE is → AnyBODY is → AnyTHING is
> No ONE is → NoBODY is → NoTHING is
> 
> "Uno", "un cuerpo", "una cosa" = SINGULAR
> ```
> 
> **Tip 3: Tachar frases preposicionales**
> 
> ```
> Paso 1: Identifica la frase preposicional
> Paso 2: Táchala mentalmente
> Paso 3: El sujeto real queda claro
> 
> Ejemplo:
> "One of the students _____ absent."
> 
> Tacha: "of the students"
> Queda: "One _____ absent."
> One = singular → is
> ```
> 
> **Tip 4: EACH = SINGULAR siempre**
> 
> ```
> EACH siempre es singular, incluso con "of":
> 
> • Each student HAS (not have)
> • Each of the students HAS
> • Each person IS
> • Each of them IS
> 
> Piensa: EACH = cada UNO (singular)
> ```
> 
> **Tip 5: Para OR/NOR - mira el más cercano**
> 
> ```
> El verbo "mira" al sujeto más cercano:
> 
> Tom or Mary IS → Mary está cerca → singular
> Tom or his friends ARE → friends está cerca → plural
> 
> Truco visual:
> Subject1 or SUBJECT2 (verb agrees with 2)
> ```
> 
> **Tip 6: Para There is/are**
> 
> ```
> There + verbo + SUJETO REAL
> 
> Paso 1: Ignora "there"
> Paso 2: Mira qué viene después
> Paso 3: Singular/uncountable → is / Plural → are
> 
> There IS a book (singular)
> There ARE books (plural)
> There IS water (uncountable)
> There ARE many people (plural)
> ```

## 🎓 Summary - Resumen

> [!summary]- Key Points to Remember
> 
> **Basic Rules:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ Singular subject → singular verb (add -s/-es to verb)
> ✓ Plural subject → plural verb (base form, no -s)
> ✓ Only HE/SHE/IT adds -s/-es in present simple
> ✓ I/YOU/WE/THEY use base form
> 
> Examples:
> • He workS, She studIES, It goES
> • I work, You work, We work, They work
> ```
> 
> **The verb "BE" (memorize!):**
> 
> ```
> Present: I am, You are, He/She/It is, We are, They are
> Past: I was, You were, He/She/It was, We were, They were
> ```
> 
> **Indefinite Pronouns:**
> 
> ```
> SINGULAR (use is/was/has/verb+s):
> everyone, everybody, everything
> someone, somebody, something
> anyone, anybody, anything
> no one, nobody, nothing
> each, every, either, neither
> 
> PLURAL (use are/were/have/base verb):
> both, few, many, several, others
> 
> DEPENDS on noun after "of":
> all, any, most, none, some
> ```
> 
> **Compound Subjects:**
> 
> ```
> • AND → plural verb
>   (Tom and Mary ARE)
> 
> • OR/NOR → verb agrees with NEAREST subject
>   (Tom or his friends ARE)
>   (His friends or Tom IS)
> 
> • As well as, along with → verb agrees with FIRST subject
>   (Tom, as well as his friends, IS)
> ```
> 
> **Special Cases:**
> 
> ```
> • Prepositional phrases → ignore, find main subject
> • There is/are → verb agrees with what follows
> • Collective nouns → usually singular in American English
> • Police, people, cattle → always plural
> • Titles, amounts, measurements → singular
> • News, mathematics, physics → singular
> • Scissors, pants, glasses → plural
> ```
> 
> **Critical for Spanish speakers:**
> 
> ```
> ✗ Common errors:
> • The students studies → works
> • Everyone are → is
> • There is many → are
> • She don't → doesn't
> • He work → works
> • One of them are → is
> • The police is → are
> 
> Remember: English agreement is "backwards" from Spanish!
> Plural subject = NO -s on verb
> Singular (he/she/it) = ADD -s to verb
> ```

## 🔗 Referencias y Conexiones

> [!quote]- Enlaces Conceptuales
> 
> **📜 Base fundamental:**
> 
> **[[03 - Plurals and Articles]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión crítica: Subject-verb agreement DEPENDE de plurals:
> 
> • Saber si el sujeto es singular o plural determina el verbo
> • "The student works" (singular) vs "The students work" (plural)
> • Irregular plurals requieren cuidado especial:
>   - "The child is" vs "The children are"
>   - "The man has" vs "The men have"
> • Uncountable nouns = always singular verb:
>   - "Water is essential" (NOT "are")
>   - "Information is important" (NOT "are")
> 
> Sin dominar plurales, NO puedes hacer agreement correctamente
> ```
> 
> **[[01 - Regular and Irregular Verbs]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión: Agreement afecta cómo conjugas los verbos:
> 
> • Present simple conjugation DEPENDE de agreement:
>   - I work, You work, He workS (adds -s)
>   - They work (no -s)
> 
> • Past simple es MÁS FÁCIL - no hay agreement:
>   - I worked, He worked, They worked (todos iguales)
> 
> • Irregular verbs también siguen agreement en presente:
>   - I go, He goES
>   - I have, She haS
>   - I do, He doES
> 
> Próximo paso: Entender cómo se conjugan los verbos completamente
> ```
> 
> **[[01 - Parts of Speech (POS)]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión: Agreement une SUBJECT (noun/pronoun) y VERB:
> 
> • Necesitas identificar el SUJETO (noun o pronoun):
>   - Proper noun: "John works"
>   - Common noun: "The teacher works"
>   - Pronoun: "He works"
> 
> • El VERBO debe concordar con ese sujeto
> 
> • Otras partes de la oración NO afectan agreement:
>   - Adjectives: "The tall student works" (student = subject)
>   - Adverbs: "She always works" (she = subject)
>   - Prepositional phrases: ignore para encontrar sujeto
> 
> Foundation crítica: Identificar partes de la oración correctamente
> ```
> 
> **[[02 - English Alphabet and Pronunciation]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión: Pronunciation afecta cómo pronuncias las terminaciones:
> 
> • Verb + S tiene tres pronunciaciones:
>   - /s/ después de sonido sordo: workS /wɜːrks/
>   - /z/ después de sonido sonoro: playS /pleɪz/
>   - /ɪz/ después de s,z,sh,ch,x: watchES /wɑːtʃɪz/
> 
> • Similar a plural -s pronunciation
> • Entender sonidos sordos/sonoros ayuda con pronunciación correcta
> ```
> 
> **Construcción de oraciones:**
> 
> ```
> Agreement es ESENCIAL para oraciones correctas:
> 
> SUBJECT + VERB (must agree!)
> 
> Sin agreement correcto:
> ✗ "The students studies English" (error básico)
> ✗ "Everyone are ready" (error común)
> ✗ "There is many books" (error frecuente)
> 
> Con agreement correcto:
> ✓ "The students study English"
> ✓ "Everyone is ready"
> ✓ "There are many books"
> 
> Este es uno de los conceptos MÁS importantes del inglés básico
> ```
