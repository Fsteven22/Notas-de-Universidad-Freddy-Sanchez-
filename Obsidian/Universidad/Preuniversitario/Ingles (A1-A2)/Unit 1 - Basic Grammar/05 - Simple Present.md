# 🎯 Simple Present Tense

## 🌟 What Is the Simple Present?

> [!info] Definition **The simple present (also called present simple) is a verb tense used to describe habits, routines, general truths, facts, and permanent situations. It's one of the most common and important tenses in English, forming the foundation for everyday communication.**
> 
> **Key points:**
> 
> - **Main use:** Habits, routines, repeated actions
> - **Also used for:** Facts, general truths, permanent situations
> - **Form:** Base verb (add -s/-es for he/she/it)
> - **Time expressions:** always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day
> - **Not used for:** Actions happening right now (use present continuous instead)
> - Essential for describing daily life and expressing regular activities

### 📖 Historical Context

> [!note]- Development of the Simple Present
> 
> **Old English period (450-1100 AD):**
> 
> - **Complex conjugation system:** Different endings for each person
> - **Example - "lufian" (to love):**
>     
>     ```
>     I lufie          We lufiaþYou lufast       You lufiaþHe/She lufaþ     They lufiaþ
>     ```
>     
> - **Multiple endings:** -e, -ast, -aþ, -iaþ
> - **Clear person markers:** Each form distinctly different
> - **No separate continuous form:** One present tense did everything
> 
> **Middle English period (1100-1500):**
> 
> - **Simplification continues:** Endings begin to merge
> - **-eth becomes dominant:** Third person singular (he loveth)
> - **Regional variations:** Northern dialects use -es instead of -eth
> - **Plural forms simplify:** All plurals use same ending (-en, later -e)
> - **Continuous forms emerge:** "I am working" starts appearing
> 
> **Early Modern English (1500-1700):**
> 
> - **-s replaces -eth:** Modern form emerges
>     - Old: "He loveth" → New: "He loves"
> - **Shakespeare's era:** Transition period, both forms used
>     - "He speaks" (becoming standard)
>     - "He speaketh" (older, formal, poetic)
> - **Other endings disappear:** Only third person singular keeps inflection
> - **Present simple vs. continuous distinction:** Becomes clearer
> 
> **Modern English (1700-present):**
> 
> - **Current system established:** Very simple pattern
>     
>     ```
>     I/You/We/They + base verbHe/She/It + base verb + s/es
>     ```
>     
> - **Only one inflection:** The -s/-es ending
> - **Negative and question forms:** Developed with DO/DOES
> - **Clear tense distinctions:** Simple present vs. present continuous
> - **Result:** One of the simplest present tense systems in European languages

## 📐 Form and Structure

### ✅ Affirmative Form

> [!success]- How to Form Affirmative Sentences
> 
> **Basic structure:**
> 
> ```
> Subject + base verb (+ s/es for he/she/it)
> 
> I/You/We/They + WORK
> He/She/It + WORKS
> ```
> 
> **Regular verbs - adding -s/-es:**
> 
> ```
> Most verbs: add -S
> • work → works
> • play → plays
> • eat → eats
> • sleep → sleeps
> • read → reads
> • write → writes
> • speak → speaks
> 
> Verbs ending in -s, -ss, -x, -z, -ch, -sh: add -ES
> • pass → passes
> • kiss → kisses
> • fix → fixes
> • buzz → buzzes
> • watch → watches
> • wash → washes
> • teach → teaches
> 
> Verbs ending in consonant + y: change Y to IES
> • study → studies
> • try → tries
> • fly → flies
> • cry → cries
> • worry → worries
> • carry → carries
> 
> Verbs ending in vowel + y: just add -S
> • play → plays
> • say → says
> • enjoy → enjoys
> • buy → buys
> • stay → stays
> 
> Verbs ending in -o: add -ES
> • go → goes
> • do → does
> • echo → echoes
> 
> BUT: also → alos (just -s)
> ```
> 
> **Irregular verbs:**
> 
> ```
> HAVE → HAS
> • I have           • He/She/It has
> • You have         • We have
> • They have
> 
> BE (completely irregular):
> • I am             • He/She/It is
> • You are          • We are
> • They are
> ```
> 
> **Complete examples:**
> 
> ```
> All persons with WORK:
> I work every day.
> You work every day.
> He works every day.
> She works every day.
> It works well.
> We work every day.
> They work every day.
> 
> All persons with STUDY:
> I study English.
> You study English.
> He studies English.
> She studies English.
> It studies patterns. (rare, but grammatical)
> We study English.
> They study English.
> ```

### ❌ Negative Form

> [!warning]- How to Form Negative Sentences
> 
> **Structure:**
> 
> ```
> Subject + DO/DOES + NOT + base verb
> 
> I/You/We/They + DO NOT (don't) + base verb
> He/She/It + DOES NOT (doesn't) + base verb
> 
> IMPORTANT: Main verb stays in BASE FORM (no -s)!
> ```
> 
> **Examples:**
> 
> ```
> Positive → Negative:
> 
> I work → I do not work / I don't work
> You play → You do not play / You don't play
> He studies → He does not study / He doesn't study
> She goes → She does not go / She doesn't go
> It works → It does not work / It doesn't work
> We like → We do not like / We don't like
> They eat → They do not eat / They don't eat
> ```
> 
> **Common mistake:**
> 
> ```
> ✗ He doesn't works → ✓ He doesn't work
> ✗ She don't like → ✓ She doesn't like
> ✗ It doesn't goes → ✓ It doesn't go
> 
> Remember: DOES carries the -s, not the main verb!
> ```
> 
> **BE verb (different pattern):**
> 
> ```
> No DO/DOES needed - just add NOT:
> 
> I am not / I'm not
> You are not / You aren't / You're not
> He is not / He isn't / He's not
> She is not / She isn't / She's not
> It is not / It isn't / It's not
> We are not / We aren't / We're not
> They are not / They aren't / They're not
> 
> Examples:
> • I am not tired.
> • She isn't happy.
> • They aren't at home.
> ```
> 
> **HAVE (can use two patterns):**
> 
> ```
> Modern (more common):
> • I don't have time.
> • He doesn't have money.
> 
> Traditional (British, formal):
> • I haven't time.
> • He hasn't money.
> ```

### ❓ Question Form

> [!info]- How to Form Questions
> 
> **Yes/No Questions:**
> 
> ```
> DO/DOES + subject + base verb?
> 
> Do I/you/we/they + base verb?
> Does he/she/it + base verb?
> ```
> 
> **Examples:**
> 
> ```
> Statement → Question:
> 
> You work → Do you work?
> He studies → Does he study?
> They live here → Do they live here?
> She likes coffee → Does she like coffee?
> It works → Does it work?
> 
> Short answers:
> Do you work? → Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
> Does she study? → Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.
> Do they like it? → Yes, they do. / No, they don't.
> ```
> 
> **WH- Questions:**
> 
> ```
> WH-word + DO/DOES + subject + base verb?
> 
> What do you do?
> Where does she work?
> When do they arrive?
> Why does he study English?
> How do you go to work?
> Who do you know? (who = object)
> 
> BUT: Who/What as subject (no DO/DOES):
> Who works here? (NOT "Who does work here?")
> What happens next? (NOT "What does happen next?")
> ```
> 
> **BE verb questions (different pattern):**
> 
> ```
> BE + subject?
> 
> Am I late?
> Are you ready?
> Is he/she/it here?
> Are we correct?
> Are they coming?
> 
> WH- questions with BE:
> Where are you?
> What is your name?
> Who is she?
> How are they?
> ```

## 🕐 Uses of Simple Present

### 1️⃣ Habits and Routines

> [!success]- Regular, Repeated Actions
> 
> **Main use:** Actions we do regularly or repeatedly
> 
> ```
> Daily routines:
> • I wake up at 7 AM every day.
> • She brushes her teeth twice a day.
> • We eat breakfast at 8 o'clock.
> • He goes to work by bus.
> • They exercise every morning.
> 
> Habits:
> • I drink coffee in the morning.
> • She reads before bed.
> • He plays soccer on weekends.
> • We watch TV in the evening.
> • They visit their parents on Sundays.
> 
> Frequency:
> • I always study English.
> • She usually arrives on time.
> • He often forgets his keys.
> • We sometimes go to the cinema.
> • They never eat fast food.
> ```
> 
> **Time expressions used:**
> 
> ```
> Frequency adverbs:
> • always (100%)
> • usually (80%)
> • often / frequently (60%)
> • sometimes (40%)
> • occasionally (20%)
> • rarely / seldom (10%)
> • never (0%)
> 
> Time phrases:
> • every day/week/month/year
> • once a day/week/month
> • twice a week
> • three times a month
> • on Mondays
> • in the morning/afternoon/evening
> • at night
> ```

### 2️⃣ General Truths and Facts

> [!info]- Permanent and Universal Facts
> 
> **For:** Things that are always true
> 
> ```
> Scientific facts:
> • Water boils at 100°C.
> • The Earth revolves around the Sun.
> • Ice melts at 0°C.
> • Plants need sunlight to grow.
> • Cats are mammals.
> 
> General truths:
> • The sun rises in the east.
> • Money doesn't grow on trees.
> • Practice makes perfect.
> • Time flies when you're having fun.
> • Honesty is the best policy.
> 
> Mathematical facts:
> • Two plus two equals four.
> • Ten divided by two is five.
> • A triangle has three sides.
> ```

### 3️⃣ Permanent Situations

> [!note]- Long-lasting or Permanent States
> 
> **For:** Situations that are stable and long-term
> 
> ```
> Personal information:
> • I live in Guayaquil.
> • She works as a teacher.
> • He comes from Spain.
> • We speak Spanish.
> • They have two children.
> 
> Permanent characteristics:
> • I like chocolate.
> • She loves music.
> • He plays the piano.
> • The house has three bedrooms.
> • This phone costs $500.
> 
> Jobs and occupations:
> • I work at a bank.
> • She teaches English.
> • He drives a taxi.
> • They own a restaurant.
> ```

### 4️⃣ Schedules and Timetables

> [!important]- Fixed Future Events
> 
> **For:** Events on a fixed schedule (not personal plans)
> 
> ```
> Transportation:
> • The bus leaves at 8 AM.
> • The train arrives at 10:30.
> • The plane departs at 6 PM.
> • The ferry sails every hour.
> 
> Events and programs:
> • The movie starts at 7 o'clock.
> • The store opens at 9 AM.
> • The class begins on Monday.
> • The concert finishes at 11 PM.
> • School starts in September.
> 
> Regular schedules:
> • The bank closes at 5 PM.
> • We have English class on Tuesdays.
> • The meeting takes place every Friday.
> ```

### 5️⃣ Stative Verbs (Non-action Verbs)

> [!warning]- Verbs Rarely Used in Continuous Form
> 
> **Stative verbs:** Describe states, not actions - usually use simple present
> 
> ```
> Mental/emotional states:
> • know: I know the answer.
> • understand: She understands English.
> • believe: They believe in God.
> • think: I think you're right. (opinion)
> • remember: He remembers everything.
> • forget: I forget his name.
> • recognize: Do you recognize this song?
> 
> Feelings and emotions:
> • like: I like pizza.
> • love: She loves chocolate.
> • hate: He hates mornings.
> • want: They want to leave.
> • need: I need help.
> • prefer: She prefers tea.
> 
> Senses:
> • see: I see a bird. (ability to see)
> • hear: I hear music.
> • smell: It smells good.
> • taste: This tastes delicious.
> 
> Possession:
> • have: I have a car. (possess)
> • own: She owns a house.
> • belong: This book belongs to me.
> • possess: He possesses great skills.
> 
> Being/appearance:
> • be: I am tired.
> • seem: She seems happy.
> • appear: He appears confused.
> • look: You look great. (appearance)
> 
> Measurement:
> • cost: It costs $10.
> • weigh: I weigh 70 kilos.
> • measure: The room measures 5x4 meters.
> ```
> 
> **Common mistake:**
> 
> ```
> ✗ I am knowing → ✓ I know
> ✗ She is liking → ✓ She likes
> ✗ He is having a car → ✓ He has a car
> ✗ They are wanting → ✓ They want
> ✗ I am understanding → ✓ I understand
> ```

## ⚖️ Simple Present vs. Present Continuous

> [!important]- Key Differences
> 
> **SIMPLE PRESENT:**
> 
> ```
> Use for:
> ✓ Habits and routines
> ✓ General truths
> ✓ Permanent situations
> ✓ Schedules
> ✓ Stative verbs
> 
> Examples:
> • I work every day. (routine)
> • Water boils at 100°C. (fact)
> • She lives in Quito. (permanent)
> • The bus leaves at 8 AM. (schedule)
> • I know the answer. (state)
> ```
> 
> **PRESENT CONTINUOUS:**
> 
> ```
> Use for:
> ✓ Actions happening NOW
> ✓ Temporary situations
> ✓ Future plans
> ✓ Changing situations
> 
> Examples:
> • I am working now. (happening now)
> • She is living in a hotel. (temporary)
> • They are coming tomorrow. (future plan)
> • It is getting cold. (changing)
> ```
> 
> **Comparison examples:**
> 
> ```
> SIMPLE PRESENT vs. PRESENT CONTINUOUS:
> 
> • I work in a bank. (permanent job)
> • I'm working on a project. (temporary, now)
> 
> • She lives in Guayaquil. (permanent)
> • She's living with friends. (temporary)
> 
> • What do you do? (your job)
> • What are you doing? (right now)
> 
> • He drinks coffee. (habit)
> • He's drinking coffee. (right now)
> 
> • The bus leaves at 8. (schedule)
> • The bus is leaving! (happening now)
> ```

## 💡 Common Mistakes and Tips

> [!warning]- Errores Comunes para Hispanohablantes
> 
> **Error 1: Olvidar la -s con he/she/it**
> 
> ```
> ✗ She work every day. → ✓ She works every day.
> ✗ He study English. → ✓ He studies English.
> ✗ It cost $10. → ✓ It costs $10.
> ✗ My brother live in Quito. → ✓ My brother lives in Quito.
> 
> Regla: Siempre agrega -s/-es con he/she/it
> ```
> 
> **Error 2: Usar simple present para acciones en progreso**
> 
> ```
> ✗ I work right now. → ✓ I am working right now.
> ✗ She eats at the moment. → ✓ She is eating at the moment.
> ✗ What you do? (ahora) → ✓ What are you doing?
> 
> Simple present = hábitos/rutinas
> Present continuous = ahora/en este momento
> ```
> 
> **Error 3: Agregar -s al verbo principal en negativo**
> 
> ```
> ✗ He doesn't works. → ✓ He doesn't work.
> ✗ She doesn't likes it. → ✓ She doesn't like it.
> ✗ It doesn't costs much. → ✓ It doesn't cost much.
> 
> DOES lleva la -s, el verbo principal queda en forma base
> ```
> 
> **Error 4: Usar DO/DOES en preguntas con BE**
> 
> ```
> ✗ Do you are ready? → ✓ Are you ready?
> ✗ Does she is a teacher? → ✓ Is she a teacher?
> ✗ Do they are students? → ✓ Are they students?
> 
> BE no usa DO/DOES - solo invierte el orden
> ```
> 
> **Error 5: Usar continuous con stative verbs**
> 
> ```
> ✗ I am knowing the answer. → ✓ I know the answer.
> ✗ She is liking pizza. → ✓ She likes pizza.
> ✗ He is having a car. → ✓ He has a car.
> ✗ They are wanting to leave. → ✓ They want to leave.
> 
> Stative verbs (know, like, have, want, need, etc.) usan simple present
> ```
> 
> **Error 6: Confundir DO (hacer) con DO (auxiliar)**
> 
> ```
> DO como verbo principal:
> ✓ What do you do? (¿Qué haces? = profesión)
> ✓ I do my homework. (hacer la tarea)
> ✓ He does exercise. (hacer ejercicio)
> 
> DO como auxiliar (sin significado):
> ✓ Do you like it? (¿Te gusta?)
> ✓ I don't know. (No sé)
> ✓ Does she work here? (¿Trabaja ella aquí?)
> ```
> 
> **Error 7: Traducir literalmente del español**
> 
> ```
> Español: "Estoy trabajando" (presente continuo común)
> ✗ I am working (si es tu trabajo habitual)
> ✓ I work at a bank. (trabajo permanente)
> 
> Español: "¿Qué haces?" (puede ser general o ahora)
> Inglés distingue:
> • What do you do? (tu profesión/trabajo)
> • What are you doing? (en este momento)
> ```

> [!tip]- Consejos y Trucos de Memoria
> 
> **Tip 1: Memoriza "HE/SHE/IT + S"**
> 
> ```
> Regla simple: Solo estos tres agregan -s
> 
> HE works, SHE studies, IT goes
> 
> Todos los demás (I, you, we, they) usan forma base:
> I work, You work, We work, They work
> 
> Truco: "HE/SHE/IT amas la S" ❤️
> ```
> 
> **Tip 2: Frequency adverbs - orden**
> 
> ```
> Posición típica: ANTES del verbo principal
> 
> I always study.
> She usually arrives on time.
> He often forgets.
> We sometimes go out.
> They never complain.
> 
> PERO: DESPUÉS del verbo BE
> I am always tired.
> She is usually happy.
> He is never late.
> ```
> 
> **Tip 3: Para recordar cuándo usar simple present**
> 
> ```
> Piensa en las "3 H + F":
> 
> H - Habits (hábitos)
> H - How things are (cómo son las cosas - hechos)
> H - How often (qué tan seguido - rutinas)
> F - Facts (hechos/verdades)
> 
> Si describe alguna de estas 4 cosas → simple present
> ```
> 
> **Tip 4: Stative verbs - "no acción"**
> 
> ```
> Stative = estado, no acción
> No puedes "ver-ing" estos verbos activamente
> 
> Puedes: run-ning, eat-ing, work-ing (acciones físicas)
> No puedes: know-ing, like-ing, belong-ing (estados mentales)
> 
> Categorías principales:
> - Mental: know, understand, believe, think (opinion)
> - Emocional: like, love, hate, want, need, prefer
> - Posesión: have (poseer), own, belong
> - Sentidos: see, hear, smell, taste (involuntarios)
> ```
> 
> **Tip 5: DO/DOES - distribución de trabajo**
> 
> ```
> En negativo y preguntas, DO/DOES hace el "trabajo" de conjugación:
> 
> Afirmativo: He work-S (verbo conjugado)
> Negativo: He DOES-n't work (DO conjugado, verbo base)
> Pregunta: DOES he work? (DO conjugado, verbo base)
> 
> Piensa: Solo UNO puede llevar la -s
> O el verbo O el auxiliar, nunca ambos
> ```
> 
> **Tip 6: Time expressions como pista**
> 
> ```
> Estas palabras/frases → simple present:
> 
> every (day/week/month/year)
> always, usually, often, sometimes, never
> on Mondays/Tuesdays (días regulares)
> once/twice/three times a (day/week)
> in the morning/afternoon/evening
> 
> Estas palabras → present continuous:
> now, right now, at the moment
> currently, presently
> today (si es temporal)
> this week/month (si es temporal)
> ```

## 🎓 Summary - Resumen

> [!summary]- Key Points to Remember
> 
> **Form:**
> 
> ```
> AFFIRMATIVE:
> I/You/We/They + base verb
> He/She/It + base verb + S/ES
> 
> NEGATIVE:
> I/You/We/They + DON'T + base verb
> He/She/It + DOESN'T + base verb
> 
> QUESTIONS:
> DO + I/you/we/they + base verb?
> DOES + he/she/it + base verb?
> ```
> 
> **Uses:**
> 
> ```
> 1. Habits and routines
>    • I work every day.
>    • She studies English.
> 
> 2. General truths and facts
>    • Water boils at 100°C.
>    • The sun rises in the east.
> 
> 3. Permanent situations
>    • I live in Guayaquil.
>    • He works as a teacher.
> 
> 4. Schedules and timetables
>    • The train leaves at 8 AM.
>    • The store opens at 9.
> 
> 5. Stative verbs (states, not actions)
>    • I know the answer.
>    • She likes chocolate.
>    • They want to leave.
> ```
> 
> **Time expressions:**
> 
> ```
> Frequency adverbs:
> always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never
> 
> Time phrases:
> every day/week/month/year
> once/twice/three times a day
> on Mondays, in the morning, at night
> ```
> 
> **Critical rules:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ Only HE/SHE/IT adds -S in affirmative
> ✓ Use DON'T/DOESN'T for negative (verb stays base form)
> ✓ Use DO/DOES for questions (verb stays base form)
> ✓ BE verb is different (am/is/are, no DO/DOES)
> ✓ Stative verbs don't use continuous form
> ✓ Simple present ≠ Present continuous
> ```
> 
> **For Spanish speakers - Remember:**
> 
> ```
> ✗ Common errors:
> • She work → works (add -s)
> • He doesn't works → work (base form)
> • Do he speak? → Does he speak?
> • I am knowing → I know (no continuous)
> • She don't like → doesn't like
> 
> Key differences from Spanish:
> • Plural subjects = NO -s on verb (opposite!)
> • Need DO/DOES for questions/negatives
> • Simple present for habits only (not actions happening now)
> ```

## 🔗 Referencias y Conexiones

> [!quote]- Enlaces Conceptuales
> 
> **📜 Base fundamental:**
> 
> **[[04 - Subject–Verb Agreement]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión CRÍTICA: Simple present ES la aplicación práctica de agreement:
> 
> • Agreement te enseña: he/she/it + verb + S
> • Simple present lo usa: He workS, She studIES, It goES
> • Todo lo que aprendiste sobre agreement se aplica AQUÍ
> • Singular/plural determina la forma del verbo
> 
> Sin dominar agreement, NO puedes usar simple present correctamente
> ```
> 
> **[[01 - Regular and Irregular Verbs]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión: Simple present usa verbos en presente:
> 
> • Regular verbs: work, play, study (add -s/-es rules)
> • Irregular verbs mantienen irregularidades:
>   - HAVE → HAS (no "haves")
>   - GO → GOES (no "gos")
>   - DO → DOES (no "dos")
> 
> • Past simple será el siguiente tiempo verbal importante
> • Entender la forma base es crucial
> 
> Base para conjugación de todos los tiempos verbales
> ```
> 
> **[[03 - Plurals and Articles]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión: Plurales afectan qué verbo usar:
> 
> • Singular subject → verb + S
>   "The student works" (singular)
> • Plural subject → base verb
>   "The students work" (plural)
> 
> • Irregular plurals requieren cuidado:
>   "The child goes" vs "The children go"
> 
> • Articles indican si es singular o plural:
>   "A student works" (singular)
>   "Students work" (plural)
> ```
> 
> **[[02 - English Alphabet and Pronunciation]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión: Pronunciación de terminaciones -s/-es:
> 
> Tres pronunciaciones del -s:
> • /s/ después de sonido sordo: works /wɜːrks/
> • /z/ después de sonido sonoro: plays /pleɪz/
> • /ɪz/ después de s,z,sh,ch,x,j: watches /wɑːtʃɪz/
> 
> Igual que la pronunciación de plurales
> Entender IPA ayuda con pronunciación correcta
> ```
> 
> **Próximo paso lógico:**
> 
> ```
> Después de dominar simple present, el siguiente tiempo es:
> 
> [[06 - Present Continuous]]
> • Contraste directo con simple present
> • Para acciones en progreso AHORA
> • Complementa simple present perfectamente
> 
> Juntos, estos dos tiempos cubren todo el presente en inglés:
> - Simple present = hábitos, hechos, permanente
> - Present continuous = ahora, temporal, progreso
> ```
> 
> **Aplicación práctica:**
> 
> ```
> Simple present es el tiempo MÁS USADO en inglés para:
> 
> • Presentarte: "I live in Guayaquil. I work as a teacher."
> • Describir rutinas: "I wake up at 7. I go to work by bus."
> • Hablar de gustos: "I like music. I don't like coffee."
> • Hacer preguntas básicas: "Do you speak English? Where do you live?"
> 
> Es la BASE de la comunicación diaria en inglés
> ```
