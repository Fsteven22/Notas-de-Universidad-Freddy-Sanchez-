# 🎯 Personal and Possessive Pronouns

## 🌟 What Are Pronouns?

> [!info] Definition **Pronouns are words that replace nouns to avoid repetition. They are essential for natural, fluent English. Instead of saying "Maria loves Maria's dog because the dog is Maria's best friend," we use pronouns: "Maria loves her dog because it is her best friend." Understanding pronouns is crucial for both speaking and understanding English.**
> 
> **Key points:**
> 
> - **Pronouns replace nouns** to avoid repetition
> - **Different types** for different functions (subject, object, possessive)
> - English has **fewer pronouns than Spanish** (no gender for "it")
> - **"You" is both singular and plural** (unlike Spanish tú/vosotros/ustedes)
> - **"It" is used for things and animals** (unlike Spanish él/ella for objects)
> - **Case matters**: Subject pronouns vs Object pronouns vs Possessive pronouns

### 📖 Why Pronouns Are Important

> [!note]- The Foundation of Natural Communication
> 
> **Pronouns make speech natural:**
> 
> ```
> Without pronouns (repetitive and unnatural):
> "John went to John's house. John opened John's door. 
>  John's dog greeted John."
> 
> With pronouns (natural):
> "John went to his house. He opened his door. 
>  His dog greeted him."
> 
> Pronouns allow:
> • Smooth, natural conversation
> • Avoid awkward repetition
> • Connect ideas across sentences
> • Make speech more efficient
> ```
> 
> **Differences from Spanish:**
> 
> ```
> Spanish has more complexity:
> • Gender: él (male), ella (female), ello (neuter)
> • Formality: tú vs usted
> • Plural: vosotros vs ustedes
> • Subject pronouns often omitted
> 
> English is simpler in some ways:
> • Only one "it" (no gender for objects)
> • Only one "you" (singular and plural, formal and informal)
> • Subject pronouns ALWAYS required
> 
> But English has complexities too:
> • Must distinguish subject vs object forms
> • Possessive has TWO forms (adjective and pronoun)
> ```

## 👤 Subject Pronouns (Pronombres de Sujeto)

### Complete List

> [!success]- Subject Pronouns Chart
> 
> **All subject pronouns:**
> 
> ```
> Singular:
> I      /aɪ/       → Yo
> You    /juː/      → Tú, Usted
> He     /hiː/      → Él (male person)
> She    /ʃiː/      → Ella (female person)
> It     /ɪt/       → Él, Ella, Eso (things, animals, concepts)
> 
> Plural:
> We     /wiː/      → Nosotros, Nosotras
> You    /juː/      → Ustedes, Vosotros, Vosotras
> They   /ðeɪ/      → Ellos, Ellas
> ```
> 
> **Function:**
> 
> ```
> Subject pronouns are the "doer" of the action:
> 
> • I work. (Yo trabajo)
> • She studies. (Ella estudia)
> • They play. (Ellos juegan)
> 
> Subject pronouns come BEFORE the verb:
> Pattern: Subject + Verb + Object
> ```

### Detailed Usage

> [!important]- "I" - First Person Singular
> 
> **Always capitalized!**
> 
> ```
> ✓ "I am a student."
> ✓ "My friend and I went shopping."
> ✗ "i am a student." (never lowercase)
> 
> Spanish: "yo" (usually omitted)
> English: "I" (ALWAYS required)
> 
> Examples:
> • I work every day.
> • I like pizza.
> • I am from Ecuador.
> 
> ⚠️ In Spanish you can say "Soy estudiante"
> In English you MUST say "I am a student"
> ```

> [!important]- "You" - Second Person (Singular AND Plural)
> 
> **One word for all situations!**
> 
> ```
> "You" = tú, usted, vosotros, ustedes
> 
> Context determines meaning:
> • "You are nice." (talking to one person)
> • "You are nice." (talking to multiple people)
> • "You" to a friend (informal)
> • "You" to your boss (formal)
> 
> No distinction in English!
> 
> Examples:
> • You are my friend. (Tú eres mi amigo)
> • You are late. (Usted está tarde)
> • You are students. (Ustedes son estudiantes)
> 
> ⚠️ When unclear, add context:
> • "You guys are late." (informal plural)
> • "You all are late." / "Y'all are late." (Southern US)
> • "Both of you are late." (exactly two people)
> ```

> [!important]- "He" - Third Person Masculine Singular
> 
> **Only for MALE people:**
> 
> ```
> Use "he" for:
> ✓ A man
> ✓ A boy
> ✓ Male person
> 
> Do NOT use for:
> ✗ Animals (usually "it")
> ✗ Objects (always "it")
> ✗ Concepts (always "it")
> 
> Examples:
> • He is my brother.
> • He works at a bank.
> • John is here. He is my friend.
> 
> Pronunciation: /hiː/ (long "ee" sound)
> ```

> [!important]- "She" - Third Person Feminine Singular
> 
> **Only for FEMALE people:**
> 
> ```
> Use "she" for:
> ✓ A woman
> ✓ A girl
> ✓ Female person
> 
> Do NOT use for:
> ✗ Animals (usually "it")
> ✗ Objects (always "it")
> ✗ Concepts (always "it")
> 
> Special cases (traditional, less common now):
> • Countries: "France is beautiful. She..."
> • Ships: "The ship sailed. She was magnificent."
> • Cars (affectionate): "My car is old but she runs well."
> 
> Examples:
> • She is my sister.
> • She studies medicine.
> • Maria is here. She is my friend.
> 
> Pronunciation: /ʃiː/ (long "ee" sound)
> ```

> [!important]- "It" - Third Person Neuter Singular
> 
> **For things, animals, and concepts:**
> 
> ```
> Use "it" for:
> ✓ Objects: "The book is here. It is red."
> ✓ Animals: "The dog is cute. It is small."
> ✓ Concepts: "Life is good. It is beautiful."
> ✓ Weather: "It is cold."
> ✓ Time: "It is 3 o'clock."
> ✓ Distance: "It is 5 miles to town."
> ✓ Babies (when gender unknown): "The baby is crying. It is hungry."
> 
> ⚠️ CRITICAL DIFFERENCE FROM SPANISH:
> 
> Spanish assigns gender to objects:
> • "El libro es rojo. Él es viejo." (libro = masculino)
> • "La mesa es grande. Ella es nueva." (mesa = femenino)
> 
> English uses "it" for ALL objects:
> • "The book is red. It is old."
> • "The table is big. It is new."
> 
> ✗ WRONG: "The book is red. He is old."
> ✓ CORRECT: "The book is red. It is old."
> 
> Exceptions (pets with known gender):
> • "My dog is smart. He learns quickly." (male dog)
> • "My cat is cute. She is fluffy." (female cat)
> ```

> [!important]- "We" - First Person Plural
> 
> **The speaker + other people:**
> 
> ```
> "We" = nosotros, nosotras
> 
> Includes:
> • I + you: "We are friends." (tú y yo)
> • I + he/she: "We work together." (él/ella y yo)
> • I + they: "We are a team." (ellos y yo)
> • I + you + they: "We are all here." (todos nosotros)
> 
> Examples:
> • We are students.
> • We live in Ecuador.
> • My family and I = We
> 
> Note: No gender distinction in English
> (Unlike Spanish nosotros/nosotras)
> ```

> [!important]- "They" - Third Person Plural
> 
> **Multiple people, things, or animals:**
> 
> ```
> "They" = ellos, ellas
> 
> Use for:
> ✓ Multiple people: "John and Mary = They"
> ✓ Multiple things: "The books = They"
> ✓ Multiple animals: "The dogs = They"
> ✓ Mixed group: "My parents = They"
> 
> Examples:
> • They are my friends.
> • They work together.
> • The books are old. They are expensive.
> 
> Note: No gender distinction in English
> (Unlike Spanish ellos/ellas)
> 
> Modern usage - Singular "they":
> • Used for a person whose gender is unknown or non-binary
> • "Someone left their phone. They will come back for it."
> • This is becoming standard in modern English
> ```

## 👥 Object Pronouns (Pronombres de Objeto)

### Complete List

> [!success]- Object Pronouns Chart
> 
> **All object pronouns:**
> 
> ```
> Singular:
> me     /miː/      → me, mí
> you    /juː/      → te, ti, le (a usted), lo, la
> him    /hɪm/      → le, lo (a él)
> her    /hɜːr/     → le, la (a ella)
> it     /ɪt/       → le, lo, la (objeto/animal)
> 
> Plural:
> us     /ʌs/       → nos
> you    /juː/      → les, los, las (a ustedes)
> them   /ðem/      → les, los, las (a ellos/ellas)
> ```
> 
> **Function:**
> 
> ```
> Object pronouns receive the action:
> 
> • She loves me. (Ella me ama)
> • I see him. (Yo lo veo)
> • They help us. (Ellos nos ayudan)
> 
> Object pronouns come AFTER the verb or preposition:
> Pattern: Subject + Verb + Object Pronoun
> ```

### Subject vs Object Comparison

> [!important]- Critical Differences
> 
> **Complete comparison table:**
> 
> ```
> Subject → Object
> 
> I       → me
> you     → you (same!)
> he      → him
> she     → her
> it      → it (same!)
> we      → us
> you     → you (same!)
> they    → them
> 
> Changes: I/me, he/him, she/her, we/us, they/them
> Same: you, it
> ```
> 
> **Usage comparison:**
> 
> ```
> Subject (before verb):
> • I love pizza.
> • He is my friend.
> • We are students.
> 
> Object (after verb):
> • She loves me.
> • I know him.
> • They help us.
> 
> ⚠️ Common mistake:
> ✗ "Me love pizza." (wrong - "me" is object, not subject)
> ✓ "I love pizza." (correct)
> 
> ✗ "Him is my friend." (wrong)
> ✓ "He is my friend." (correct)
> ```

### Detailed Usage

> [!example]- Object Pronouns in Action
> 
> **After verbs (Direct object):**
> 
> ```
> • She loves me. (Ella me ama)
> • I see him. (Yo lo veo a él)
> • They know her. (Ellos la conocen a ella)
> • We like it. (A nosotros nos gusta)
> • He calls us. (Él nos llama)
> • I help them. (Yo los ayudo)
> 
> Pattern: Subject + Verb + Object Pronoun
> ```
> 
> **After prepositions:**
> 
> ```
> • This is for me. (Esto es para mí)
> • Come with us. (Ven con nosotros)
> • She's talking to him. (Ella está hablando con él)
> • I'm thinking about her. (Estoy pensando en ella)
> • Look at them! (¡Míralos!)
> • Between you and me... (Entre tú y yo...)
> 
> Pattern: Preposition + Object Pronoun
> 
> Common prepositions:
> for, with, to, about, at, between, without, after, before
> ```
> 
> **⚠️ Special case: "than" and "as"**
> 
> ```
> Formal (object pronoun):
> • She is taller than me.
> • He runs faster than us.
> 
> Very formal (subject pronoun + verb):
> • She is taller than I am.
> • He runs faster than we do.
> 
> Most native speakers use object pronouns after "than" in 
> everyday speech, even though subject + verb is technically 
> more grammatically correct.
> ```

## 🏠 Possessive Adjectives (Adjetivos Posesivos)

### Complete List

> [!success]- Possessive Adjectives Chart
> 
> **All possessive adjectives:**
> 
> ```
> Singular:
> my      /maɪ/      → mi, mis
> your    /jɔːr/     → tu, tus / su, sus (de usted)
> his     /hɪz/      → su, sus (de él)
> her     /hɜːr/     → su, sus (de ella)
> its     /ɪts/      → su, sus (de eso/animal)
> 
> Plural:
> our     /ˈaʊər/    → nuestro, nuestra, nuestros, nuestras
> your    /jɔːr/     → su, sus (de ustedes) / vuestro (Spain)
> their   /ðer/      → su, sus (de ellos/ellas)
> ```
> 
> **Function:**
> 
> ```
> Possessive adjectives show ownership:
> 
> • my book (mi libro)
> • her car (su carro de ella)
> • their house (su casa de ellos)
> 
> Possessive adjectives come BEFORE the noun:
> Pattern: Possessive Adjective + Noun
> 
> ⚠️ They are ADJECTIVES, not pronouns!
> They MUST be followed by a noun.
> ```

### Key Characteristics

> [!important]- Important Rules
> 
> **Rule 1: No gender agreement**
> 
> ```
> Spanish changes with the possessed object:
> • mi libro (masculine)
> • mi casa (feminine)
> • mis libros (plural)
> 
> English NEVER changes:
> • my book
> • my house
> • my books (same form!)
> 
> The possessive adjective depends on the OWNER, not the object:
> • his book (él es el dueño)
> • his house (él es el dueño)
> • her book (ella es la dueña)
> • her house (ella es la dueña)
> ```
> 
> **Rule 2: Always use them (unlike Spanish)**
> 
> ```
> Spanish can omit possessive:
> • "Me duele la cabeza" (no possessive needed)
> 
> English requires possessive:
> • "My head hurts" (must use "my")
> 
> ✗ "Head hurts" (wrong)
> ✓ "My head hurts" (correct)
> 
> Always use possessive adjectives with:
> • Body parts: my hand, your eyes, his nose
> • Clothing: my shirt, her dress, their shoes
> • Family: my mother, his brother, our family
> • Belongings: your phone, my car, our house
> ```
> 
> **Rule 3: "Its" vs "It's"**
> 
> ```
> its = possessive adjective (su, sus - de eso)
> • "The dog wagged its tail." (su cola del perro)
> 
> it's = contraction of "it is"
> • "It's cold today." (It is cold)
> 
> ⚠️ NEVER use apostrophe for possessive "its"!
> ✗ "The dog wagged it's tail." (wrong)
> ✓ "The dog wagged its tail." (correct)
> ✓ "It's a beautiful day." (It is = correct)
> ```

### Usage Examples

> [!example]- Possessive Adjectives in Context
> 
> **With body parts:**
> 
> ```
> • I wash my hands.
> • She closes her eyes.
> • He breaks his arm.
> • We brush our teeth.
> • They shake their heads.
> 
> Spanish: "Me lavo las manos" (article)
> English: "I wash my hands" (possessive)
> ```
> 
> **With family:**
> 
> ```
> • my mother, my father, my parents
> • your sister, your brother, your siblings
> • his wife, her husband
> • our grandmother, their grandfather
> • my son, her daughter, their children
> ```
> 
> **With belongings:**
> 
> ```
> • my phone, your computer, his car
> • her house, its color, our school
> • your books, their classroom
> 
> Pattern: possessive + noun
> ```
> 
> **In sentences:**
> 
> ```
> • This is my book.
> • Where is your pen?
> • His name is John.
> • Her car is red.
> • Our house is big.
> • Their dog is cute.
> 
> Questions:
> • What is your name?
> • Where is his phone?
> • How old is her brother?
> ```

## 💎 Possessive Pronouns (Pronombres Posesivos)

### Complete List

> [!success]- Possessive Pronouns Chart
> 
> **All possessive pronouns:**
> 
> ```
> Singular:
> mine     /maɪn/       → mío, mía, míos, mías
> yours    /jɔːrz/      → tuyo, tuya / suyo, suya (de usted)
> his      /hɪz/        → suyo, suya (de él)
> hers     /hɜːrz/      → suyo, suya (de ella)
> ---                   → (no possessive pronoun for "it")
> 
> Plural:
> ours     /ˈaʊərz/     → nuestro, nuestra, nuestros, nuestras
> yours    /jɔːrz/      → suyo, suya (de ustedes) / vuestro
> theirs   /ðerz/       → suyo, suya (de ellos/ellas)
> ```
> 
> **Function:**
> 
> ```
> Possessive pronouns REPLACE the noun + possessive adjective:
> 
> Possessive adjective + noun:
> • "This is my book."
> 
> Possessive pronoun (standalone):
> • "This book is mine."
> • "This is mine." (replaces "my book")
> 
> Pattern: Possessive Pronoun (NO noun after)
> ```

### Adjective vs Pronoun Comparison

> [!important]- Critical Distinction
> 
> **Possessive Adjective + Noun vs Possessive Pronoun:**
> 
> ```
> Possessive Adjective  →  Possessive Pronoun
> (+ noun)                  (no noun)
> 
> my book               →  mine
> your car              →  yours
> his phone             →  his (same form!)
> her house             →  hers
> its color             →  --- (no pronoun form)
> our dog               →  ours
> your books            →  yours
> their children        →  theirs
> ```
> 
> **Usage comparison:**
> 
> ```
> With noun (Adjective):
> • This is my book.
> • That is your car.
> • This is his phone.
> 
> Without noun (Pronoun):
> • This book is mine.
> • That car is yours.
> • This phone is his.
> 
> ⚠️ Common mistake:
> ✗ "This is mine book." (can't use both!)
> ✓ "This is my book." (adjective + noun)
> ✓ "This is mine." (pronoun alone)
> ```

### Usage Examples

> [!example]- Possessive Pronouns in Action
> 
> **Showing ownership:**
> 
> ```
> • This book is mine. (Este libro es mío)
> • That car is yours. (Ese carro es tuyo)
> • The red phone is his. (El teléfono rojo es suyo/de él)
> • The blue phone is hers. (El teléfono azul es suyo/de ella)
> • The house on the corner is ours. (La casa es nuestra)
> • Those shoes are theirs. (Esos zapatos son suyos/de ellos)
> ```
> 
> **Answering "Whose...?" questions:**
> 
> ```
> Q: Whose book is this?
> A: It's mine. / It's yours. / It's his.
> 
> Q: Whose car is that?
> A: It's hers. / It's ours. / It's theirs.
> ```
> 
> **Comparing ownership:**
> 
> ```
> • My car is old, but yours is new.
> • His house is big, hers is small.
> • Our dog is friendly, theirs is shy.
> ```
> 
> **Common expressions:**
> 
> ```
> • "A friend of mine" = un amigo mío
> • "That idea of yours" = esa idea tuya
> • "This problem of ours" = este problema nuestro
> 
> Pattern: noun + of + possessive pronoun
> ```

## 🔄 Reflexive Pronouns (Pronombres Reflexivos)

### Complete List

> [!success]- Reflexive Pronouns Chart
> 
> **All reflexive pronouns:**
> 
> ```
> Singular:
> myself      /maɪˈsɛlf/       → yo mismo/a, me
> yourself    /jɔːrˈsɛlf/      → tú mismo/a, te
> himself     /hɪmˈsɛlf/       → él mismo, se
> herself     /hɜːrˈsɛlf/      → ella misma, se
> itself      /ɪtˈsɛlf/        → sí mismo
> 
> Plural:
> ourselves   /ˌaʊərˈsɛlvz/    → nosotros mismos/as, nos
> yourselves  /jɔːrˈsɛlvz/     → ustedes mismos/as, se
> themselves  /ðəmˈsɛlvz/      → ellos/ellas mismos/as, se
> ```
> 
> **Function:**
> 
> ```
> Reflexive pronouns show that the subject does
> something to him/herself:
> 
> • I wash myself. (Me lavo)
> • She sees herself in the mirror. (Se ve en el espejo)
> ```

### Usage

> [!important]- When to Use Reflexive Pronouns
> 
> **1. Reflexive actions (subject = object):**
> 
> ```
> • I hurt myself. (Me lastimé)
> • He cut himself. (Se cortó)
> • She looked at herself. (Se miró)
> • We enjoyed ourselves. (Nos divertimos)
> • They introduced themselves. (Se presentaron)
> 
> ⚠️ IMPORTANT DIFFERENCE:
> English uses reflexive pronouns LESS than Spanish!
> 
> Spanish reflexive verbs:
> • levantarse, ducharse, vestirse, sentarse
> 
> English non-reflexive:
> • to get up, to shower, to dress, to sit down
> 
> ✗ "I wake myself up" (sounds strange)
> ✓ "I wake up" (correct - no reflexive needed)
> ```
> 
> **2. For emphasis (mismo/a):**
> 
> ```
> • I myself saw it. (Yo mismo lo vi)
> • She herself said so. (Ella misma lo dijo)
> • They themselves admitted it. (Ellos mismos lo admitieron)
> • Do it yourself! (¡Hazlo tú mismo!)
> ```
> 
> **3. With "by" (solo/a, por sí mismo/a):**
> 
> ```
> • I live by myself. (Vivo solo)
> • She did it by herself. (Lo hizo sola/por sí misma)
> • Can you do it by yourself? (¿Puedes hacerlo solo?)
> ```
> 
> **Common expressions:**
> 
> ```
> • Help yourself! (¡Sírvete!)
> • Make yourself at home. (Ponte cómodo)
> • Enjoy yourself/yourselves! (¡Diviértete/Diviértanse!)
> • Be yourself. (Sé tú mismo)
> • Take care of yourself. (Cuídate)
> ```

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

> [!warning]- Typical Errors for Spanish Speakers
> 
> **Mistake 1: Omitting subject pronouns**
> 
> ```
> Spanish: "Soy estudiante" (no pronoun needed)
> 
> ❌ WRONG: "Am student."
> ✓ CORRECT: "I am a student."
> 
> English ALWAYS needs subject pronouns!
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 2: Using object pronouns as subjects**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "Me and John went to the store."
> ✓ CORRECT: "John and I went to the store."
> 
> ❌ WRONG: "Him is my friend."
> ✓ CORRECT: "He is my friend."
> 
> Trick: Remove the other person and see if it sounds right:
> "Me went to the store" ✗ → Use "I"
> "I went to the store" ✓
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 3: Wrong pronoun for objects/animals**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "The table is big. She is new."
> ✓ CORRECT: "The table is big. It is new."
> 
> ❌ WRONG: "The book is here. He is red."
> ✓ CORRECT: "The book is here. It is red."
> 
> Use "it" for objects, NOT he/she!
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 4: Possessive adjective with wrong gender**
> 
> ```
> Spanish: "su casa" depends on speaker gender sometimes
> 
> ❌ WRONG: "María and her car" → then "his car" 
>           (changing based on car, not María)
> 
> ✓ CORRECT: Always "her car" (because María owns it)
> 
> The possessive matches the OWNER, not the possessed!
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 5: "Its" vs "It's" confusion**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "The dog wagged it's tail."
> ✓ CORRECT: "The dog wagged its tail."
> 
> ❌ WRONG: "Its cold today."
> ✓ CORRECT: "It's cold today." (It is)
> 
> Remember: its = possession (NO apostrophe)
>           it's = it is (WITH apostrophe)
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 6: Using possessive pronoun + noun**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "This is mine book."
> ✓ CORRECT: "This is my book." (adjective)
> ✓ CORRECT: "This is mine." (pronoun)
> 
> ❌ WRONG: "That is yours car."
> ✓ CORRECT: "That is your car." (adjective)
> ✓ CORRECT: "That is yours." (pronoun)
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 7: Overusing reflexive pronouns**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "I wake myself up at 7."
> ✓ CORRECT: "I wake up at 7."
> 
> ❌ WRONG: "She showers herself every day."
> ✓ CORRECT: "She showers every day."
> 
> Most Spanish reflexive verbs are NOT reflexive in English!
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 8: "Me" instead of "I" with "and"**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "Me and my friend went shopping."
> ✓ CORRECT: "My friend and I went shopping."
> 
> ❌ WRONG: "My mom took me and my sister."
> ✓ CORRECT: "My mom took my sister and me."
> 
> Rule: Use "I" for subject, "me" for object
> Also: It's polite to put the other person first!
> ```

## 🌍 Special Cases and Advanced Usage

> [!info]- Singular "They" (Modern Usage)
> 
> **Gender-neutral pronoun:**
> 
> ```
> Modern English increasingly uses "they" for:
> 
> 1. Unknown gender:
>    • "Someone left their phone here. They'll probably come back."
>    • "The doctor will see you. They will be here soon."
> 
> 2. Non-binary individuals:
>    • "This is my friend Sam. They are a teacher."
>    • "Ask Alex if they want to come."
> 
> 3. Avoiding gender assumptions:
>    • "Every student should bring their book."
>    (instead of "his or her book")
> 
> This usage is becoming standard in modern English!
> ```

> [!info]- "One" as Impersonal Pronoun
> 
> **Formal/General statements:**
> 
> ```
> "One" = a person in general (formal)
> 
> Examples:
> • "One should always be polite."
> • "One never knows what might happen."
> • "One must be careful."
> 
> Similar to Spanish "uno/una" or impersonal "se"
> 
> More common in British English
> American English prefers "you" for general statements:
> • "You should always be polite."
> • "You never know what might happen."
> ```

> [!info]- Pronoun Order in Sentences
> 
> **Multiple pronouns together:**
> 
> ```
> Rule 1: Put the other person first (politeness)
> ✓ "You and I should go."
> ✗ "I and you should go." (sounds rude)
> 
> Rule 2: When in doubt, remove the other person:
> "Me and John went" → "Me went" ✗ → Use "I"
> "John and I went" ✓
> 
> Rule 3: Object pronouns after verb:
> • "He gave it to me and you."
> • "She told them and us."
> 
> Rule 4: Different persons in order:
> 2nd person → 3rd person → 1st person
> • "You, he, and I should talk."
> • "You and I need to leave."
> ```

## 🔗 Referencias y Conexiones

> [!quote]- Enlaces Conceptuales
> 
> **📜 Fundación previa:**
> 
> **[[00 - English Alphabet and Pronunciation]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión: Pronunciación correcta de pronombres:
> 
> • "I" /aɪ/ vs "me" /miː/ - vocales diferentes
> • "he" /hiː/ vs "him" /hɪm/ - vocal larga vs corta
> • "its" /ɪts/ vs "it's" /ɪts/ - suenan igual
> • "their" /ðer/ vs "there" /ðer/ vs "they're" /ðeɪr/
> 
> Pronunciación crítica:
> - TH sound en "they", "them", "their"
> - Distinguir sonidos similares
> ```
> 
> **[[01 - Verb to be]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión: Los pronombres determinan la forma del verbo:
> 
> • I am, you are, he is, she is, it is
> • We are, you are, they are
> 
> Cada pronombre personal tiene su conjugación:
> - Subject pronouns + verb "to be"
> - "It is cold" (always need "it")
> 
> Possessive con "to be":
> • "This is my book"
> • "That is yours"
> ```
> 
> **[[03 - Numbers, Days, Months, Colors]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión: Pronombres posesivos con información personal:
> 
> • "My birthday is..." (possessive)
> • "I am 25 years old" (subject)
> • "Her favorite color is blue" (possessive)
> 
> Describir pertenencias:
> - "My phone number is..."
> - "His birthday is in March"
> - "Their favorite color is green"
> ```
> 
> **📜 Continuación inmediata:**
> 
> **[[06 - Plurals and Articles]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión: Pronombres con singular/plural:
> 
> • "He is" (singular) vs "They are" (plural)
> • "This is mine" vs "These are mine"
> • "It is a book" vs "They are books"
> 
> Articles con possessive adjectives:
> - NO usar "the" con possessive adjectives
> - ✗ "The my book"
> - ✓ "My book"
> ```
> 
> **[[07 - Subject-Verb Agreement]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión: El pronombre determina la conjugación:
> 
> • I work / He works (diferente forma)
> • We study / She studies
> • They run / It runs
> 
> Los pronombres son el sujeto:
> - Subject pronouns + verb conjugation
> - "He goes" (3rd person singular)
> - "They go" (3rd person plural)
> ```
> 
> **[[13 - Parts of Speech]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión: Los pronombres son una parte de la oración:
> 
> Pronouns replace nouns:
> • "John loves Mary" → "He loves her"
> 
> Different types of pronouns:
> - Personal (I, you, he)
> - Possessive (my, mine)
> - Reflexive (myself, yourself)
> - Demonstrative (this, that)
> - Relative (who, which, that)
> ```

## 🎓 Quick Reference Card

> [!tip]- Cheat Sheet: Pronouns
> 
> **Subject vs Object:**
> 
> ```
> SUBJECT (before verb)  OBJECT (after verb)
> I                      me
> you                    you
> he                     him
> she                    her
> it                     it
> we                     us
> they                   them
> ```
> 
> **Possessive Adjective vs Pronoun:**
> 
> ```
> ADJECTIVE (+noun)      PRONOUN (alone)
> my book                mine
> your car               yours
> his phone              his
> her house              hers
> its tail               ---
> our dog                ours
> their cat              theirs
> ```
> 
> **Common Mistakes to Avoid:**
> 
> ```
> ✗ Me love pizza        ✓ I love pizza
> ✗ Him is tall          ✓ He is tall
> ✗ The book is he       ✓ The book is his
> ✗ This is mine book    ✓ This is my book
> ✗ Its cold             ✓ It's cold (it is)
> ✗ The dog wagged it's tail  ✓ ...its tail
> ✗ Me and John          ✓ John and I
> ```
> 
> **Remember:**
> 
> ```
> • Subject pronouns = before verb
> • Object pronouns = after verb/preposition
> • Possessive adjective = with noun
> • Possessive pronoun = without noun
> • "It" for objects/animals
> • "Its" = possessive (no apostrophe)
> • "It's" = it is (with apostrophe)
> ```
