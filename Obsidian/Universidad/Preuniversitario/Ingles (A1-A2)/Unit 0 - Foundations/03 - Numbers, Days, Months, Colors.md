# 🎯 Numbers, Days, Months, Colors

## 🌟 What Are These Foundational Elements?

> [!info] Definition **Numbers, days, months, and colors are essential vocabulary for daily communication in English. They help you tell time, make appointments, describe things, count, and organize your life. These are some of the most frequently used words in English, so mastering them early is crucial.**
> 
> **Key points:**
> 
> - **Numbers** = Used for counting, prices, phone numbers, addresses, ages
> - **Days** = Seven days of the week (Monday - Sunday)
> - **Months** = Twelve months of the year (January - December)
> - **Colors** = Describing appearance, objects, feelings, preferences
> - All require **correct pronunciation** and **spelling**
> - Understanding these enables you to handle daily situations
> - Foundation for telling time, dates, and descriptions

### 📖 Cultural Context

> [!note]- Cultural Differences in Numbers and Dates
> 
> **Date Format Differences:**
> 
> ```
> United States:
> • MM/DD/YYYY (Month/Day/Year)
> • Example: 10/01/2025 = October 1st, 2025
> 
> Most other English-speaking countries:
> • DD/MM/YYYY (Day/Month/Year)
> • Example: 01/10/2025 = October 1st, 2025
> 
> ⚠️ Potential confusion:
> 05/06/2025 could be:
> • US: May 6th, 2025
> • UK: June 5th, 2025
> ```
> 
> **Number Symbols:**
> 
> ```
> Decimal separator:
> • US/UK: period (.) → 3.14
> • Spanish: comma (,) → 3,14
> 
> Thousands separator:
> • US/UK: comma (,) → 1,000,000
> • Spanish: period (.) → 1.000.000
> 
> Example:
> English: $1,500.75 (one thousand five hundred dollars and 75 cents)
> Spanish: $1.500,75
> ```
> 
> **Week Start Day:**
> 
> ```
> United States: Week starts on Sunday
> Most other countries: Week starts on Monday
> 
> This affects calendars and scheduling!
> ```

### 21-100 (Tens and Combinations)

> [!success]- Tens and How to Form Numbers
> 
> **The tens (20, 30, 40...):**
> 
> ```
> 20 = twenty      /ˈtwɛnti/
> 30 = thirty      /ˈθɜːrti/
> 40 = forty       /ˈfɔːrti/         ⚠️ Note: NOT "fourty"!
> 50 = fifty       /ˈfɪfti/
> 60 = sixty       /ˈsɪksti/
> 70 = seventy     /ˈsɛvənti/
> 80 = eighty      /ˈeɪti/
> 90 = ninety      /ˈnaɪnti/
> 100 = one hundred /wʌn ˈhʌndrəd/
> ```
> 
> **Pattern for 21-99:**
> 
> ```
> Rule: [tens] + hyphen + [ones]
> 
> Examples:
> 21 = twenty-one
> 35 = thirty-five
> 42 = forty-two
> 58 = fifty-eight
> 67 = sixty-seven
> 73 = seventy-three
> 84 = eighty-four
> 99 = ninety-nine
> 
> ⚠️ Important: Use hyphen in writing!
> ```
> 
> **Pronunciation tip - Stress patterns:**
> 
> ```
> TEENS (13-19): Stress on SECOND syllable
> • thirTEEN /θɜːrˈtiːn/
> • fourTEEN /fɔːrˈtiːn/
> • fifTEEN /fɪfˈtiːn/
> 
> TENS (20, 30, 40...): Stress on FIRST syllable
> • THIRty /ˈθɜːrti/
> • FORty /ˈfɔːrti/
> • FIFty /ˈfɪfti/
> 
> This helps distinguish:
> • "I'm thirTEEN" (13 years old)
> • "I'm THIRty" (30 years old)
> ```

>

> 456 = four hundred fifty-six (no "and" in American English)

> ```
> 
> **Thousands:**
> 
> ```
> 1,000 = one thousand (or "a thousand")
> 2,000 = two thousand
> 10,000 = ten thousand
> 25,000 = twenty-five thousand

> ```
> 
> **Millions and beyond:**
> 
> ```
> 1,000,000 = one million
> 10,000,000 = ten million
> 100,000,000 = one hundred million
> 1,000,000,000 = one billion
> 1,000,000,000,000 = one trillion
> 
> Examples in sentences:
> • "The population is 17 million."
> • "The company earned 5.3 billion dollars."
> • "There are 7 billion people on Earth."

>

> 
> ```
> 2,547 = 
> "two thousand, five hundred forty-seven"

> 
> 1,234,567 = 
> "one million, two hundred thirty-four thousand, 
>  five hundred sixty-seven"
> 
> Pattern: Break into groups of three from right to left
> ```

## 🔢 Ordinal Numbers

### 1st - 31st

> [!success]- Ordinal Numbers (Order/Position)
> 
> **First 20 ordinals:**
> 
> ```
> 1st  = first       /fɜːrst/
> 2nd  = second      /ˈsɛkənd/
> 3rd  = third       /θɜːrd/
> 4th  = fourth      /fɔːrθ/
> 5th  = fifth       /fɪfθ/
> 6th  = sixth       /sɪksθ/
> 7th  = seventh     /ˈsɛvənθ/
> 8th  = eighth      /eɪtθ/
> 9th  = ninth       /naɪnθ/
> 10th = tenth       /tɛnθ/
> 11th = eleventh    /ɪˈlɛvənθ/
> 12th = twelfth     /twɛlfθ/
> 13th = thirteenth  /ˌθɜːrˈtiːnθ/
> 14th = fourteenth  /ˌfɔːrˈtiːnθ/
> 15th = fifteenth   /ˌfɪfˈtiːnθ/
> 16th = sixteenth   /ˌsɪksˈtiːnθ/
> 17th = seventeenth /ˌsɛvənˈtiːnθ/
> 18th = eighteenth  /ˌeɪˈtiːnθ/
> 19th = nineteenth  /ˌnaɪnˈtiːnθ/
> 20th = twentieth   /ˈtwɛntiəθ/
> ```
> 
> **Pattern for ordinals:**
> 
> ```
> Most numbers: add "-th"
> • four → fourth
> • seven → seventh
> • ten → tenth
> 
> Exceptions (irregular):
> • one → first (not "oneth")
> • two → second (not "twoth")
> • three → third (not "threeth")
> • five → fifth (not "fiveth")
> • eight → eighth (not "eightth")
> • nine → ninth (not "nineth" - drops 'e')
> • twelve → twelfth (not "twelveth")
> ```
> 
> **For numbers ending in -ty:**
> 
> ```
> Change -y to -ieth:
> 
> 20 = twenty  → 20th = twentieth
> 30 = thirty  → 30th = thirtieth
> 40 = forty   → 40th = fortieth
> 50 = fifty   → 50th = fiftieth
> 60 = sixty   → 60th = sixtieth
> 70 = seventy → 70th = seventieth
> 80 = eighty  → 80th = eightieth
> 90 = ninety  → 90th = ninetieth
> ```
> 
> **Combined ordinals:**
> 
> ```
> Only the LAST number becomes ordinal:
> 
> 21st = twenty-first (not "twenty-oneth")
> 22nd = twenty-second
> 23rd = twenty-third
> 24th = twenty-fourth
> 31st = thirty-first
> 52nd = fifty-second
> 83rd = eighty-third
> 99th = ninety-ninth
> ```
> 
> **Usage:**
> 
> ```
> Dates:
> • "October 1st" (October first)
> • "December 25th" (December twenty-fifth)
> 
> Floors:
> • "I live on the 5th floor"
> 
> Position/Rank:
> • "She finished in 1st place"
> • "He came in 3rd"
> 
> Fractions:
> • "One third" (1/3)
> • "Two fifths" (2/5)
> ```

## 📅 Days of the Week

### The Seven Days

> [!success]- Days of the Week with Pronunciation
> 
> **Complete list:**
> 
> ```
> Monday    /ˈmʌndeɪ/     (Lunes)
> Tuesday   /ˈtuːzdeɪ/    (Martes)
> Wednesday /ˈwɛnzdeɪ/    (Miércoles)
> Thursday  /ˈθɜːrzdeɪ/   (Jueves)
> Friday    /ˈfraɪdeɪ/    (Viernes)
> Saturday  /ˈsætərdeɪ/   (Sábado)
> Sunday    /ˈsʌndeɪ/     (Domingo)
> ```
> 
> **Important rules:**
> 
> ```
> 1. Always CAPITALIZE days in English!
>    ✓ Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday
>    ✗ monday, tuesday, wednesday
> 
> 2. Never use articles with days for routines:
>    ✓ "I work on Monday"
>    ✓ "See you Friday"
>    ✗ "I work on the Monday"
> 
> 3. Use "on" with specific days:
>    ✓ "on Monday"
>    ✓ "on Friday afternoon"
>    ✓ "on Sunday morning"
> ```
> 
> **Abbreviations:**
> 
> ```
> Standard (3 letters):
> Mon. / Tue. / Wed. / Thu. / Fri. / Sat. / Sun.
> 
> Two letters (informal):
> Mo / Tu / We / Th / Fr / Sa / Su
> 
> Single letter (calendars):
> M / T / W / T / F / S / S
> (Note: Two T's for Tuesday and Thursday!)
> ```
> 
> **Pronunciation challenges:**
> 
> ```
> Wednesday /ˈwɛnzdeɪ/:
> ⚠️ The 'd' is silent or very soft!
> ✗ "wed-nes-day" (spelling pronunciation)
> ✓ "wenz-day" (actual pronunciation)
> 
> Thursday /ˈθɜːrzdeɪ/:
> ⚠️ Starts with TH sound /θ/
> ✗ "turs-day"
> ✓ "thurs-day"
> 
> Tuesday /ˈtuːzdeɪ/:
> US: "tooz-day"
> UK: "tyooz-day"
> ```

### Common Expressions with Days

> [!important]- Useful Phrases
> 
> **Talking about today and relative days:**
> 
> ```
> today        = hoy
> yesterday    = ayer
> tomorrow     = mañana
> 
> the day before yesterday = anteayer
> the day after tomorrow   = pasado mañana
> 
> this morning    = esta mañana
> this afternoon  = esta tarde
> this evening    = esta noche
> tonight         = esta noche
> 
> last night      = anoche
> last week       = la semana pasada
> next week       = la próxima semana
> ```
> 
> **Specific day expressions:**
> 
> ```
> With "on" (specific day):
> • "on Monday" = el lunes
> • "on Friday evening" = el viernes por la noche
> • "on Sunday morning" = el domingo por la mañana
> 
> Without "on" (every/habitually):
> • "Mondays" = los lunes (every Monday)
> • "I work Saturdays" = Trabajo los sábados
> • "We have class Tuesdays and Thursdays"
> 
> With "this/next/last":
> • "this Monday" = este lunes
> • "next Friday" = el próximo viernes
> • "last Wednesday" = el miércoles pasado
> ```
> 
> **Weekend expressions:**
> 
> ```
> the weekend    = el fin de semana
> this weekend   = este fin de semana
> last weekend   = el fin de semana pasado
> next weekend   = el próximo fin de semana
> 
> on the weekend = en el fin de semana (US: "on the weekend")
> at the weekend = en el fin de semana (UK: "at the weekend")
> 
> over the weekend = durante el fin de semana
> 
> Examples:
> • "What are you doing this weekend?"
> • "I went to the beach last weekend."
> • "Let's meet on Saturday."
> ```
> 
> **Days of the week categories:**
> 
> ```
> Weekdays = Monday - Friday (días laborables)
> • "I work on weekdays."
> • "Weekdays are busy for me."
> 
> Weekend = Saturday - Sunday (fin de semana)
> • "I relax on the weekend."
> • "Weekends are for family."
> ```

### Writing and Saying Dates

> [!important]- Date Formats
> 
> **Writing dates:**
> 
> ```
> American format: Month/Day/Year
> • 10/01/2025 = October 1, 2025
> • 12/25/2025 = December 25, 2025
> 
> British format: Day/Month/Year
> • 01/10/2025 = 1 October 2025
> • 25/12/2025 = 25 December 2025
> 
> International (ISO): Year-Month-Day
> • 2025-10-01 = October 1, 2025
> • 2025-12-25 = December 25, 2025
> ```
> 
> **Saying dates out loud:**
> 
> ```
> American style:
> "October first, twenty twenty-five"
> "December twenty-fifth, twenty twenty-five"
> Pattern: [Month] + [ordinal day], [year]
> 
> British style:
> "The first of October, twenty twenty-five"
> "The twenty-fifth of December, twenty twenty-five"
> Pattern: The [ordinal day] of [Month], [year]
> ```
> 
> **Writing dates formally:**
> 
> ```
> American:
> • October 1, 2025
> • October 1st, 2025 (with ordinal indicator)
> • Oct. 1, 2025 (abbreviated)
> 
> British:
> • 1 October 2025
> • 1st October 2025
> • 1 Oct. 2025
> 
> Common in both:
> • 1/10/25 (short form - but means different dates!)
> • 10-01-2025
> ```
> 
> **Asking about dates:**
> 
> ```
> "What's the date today?"
> → "It's October 1st."
> 
> "What date is it?"
> → "It's the first."
> 
> "When is your birthday?"
> → "March 15th" or "The fifteenth of March"
> 
> "What day is today?"
> → "It's Monday." (asking for day of week, not date!)
> ```

## 📝 Common Mistakes to Avoid

> [!warning]- Typical Errors for Spanish Speakers
> 
> **Mistake 1: Not using hyphen in compound numbers**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "twenty one", "thirty five"
> ✓ CORRECT: "twenty-one", "thirty-five"
> 
> Rule: Always use hyphen for numbers 21-99
> (except 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90)
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 2: Wrong stress in teens vs tens**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "I'm THIRteen" (sounds like 30)
> ✓ CORRECT: "I'm thirTEEN" (13)
> 
> ❌ WRONG: "I need fifTEEN dollars" (sounds like 50)
> ✓ CORRECT: "I need FIFteen dollars"
> 
> Remember: TEENS = stress on second syllable
>           TENS = stress on first syllable
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 3: Spelling "forty" as "fourty"**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "fourty"
> ✓ CORRECT: "forty"
> 
> Note: This is the only number where the 'u' 
> from 'four' is dropped!
> 
> • four → fourth (4th) ✓
> • four → fourteen (14) ✓
> • four → forty (40) ✓ (no 'u'!)
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 4: Not capitalizing days and months**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "See you on monday in january"
> ✓ CORRECT: "See you on Monday in January"
> 
> Spanish: days and months are lowercase
> English: days and months are ALWAYS capitalized
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 5: Wrong preposition with days/months**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "in Monday", "at Friday"
> ✓ CORRECT: "on Monday", "on Friday"
> 
> ❌ WRONG: "on January", "at March"
> ✓ CORRECT: "in January", "in March"
> 
> Rule: Use "on" with days, "in" with months
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 6: Using article with days (routine)**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "I work on the Monday"
> ✓ CORRECT: "I work on Monday"
> 
> ❌ WRONG: "I have class the Tuesdays"
> ✓ CORRECT: "I have class on Tuesdays" (every Tuesday)
> ✓ CORRECT: "I have class Tuesdays" (also acceptable)
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 7: Date format confusion**
> 
> ```
> 05/06/2025 could mean:
> • US: May 6th, 2025
> • UK/Others: June 5th, 2025
> 
> Solution: Write month name to avoid confusion
> ✓ "June 5, 2025" or "5 June 2025"
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 8: Translating "de nada" for numbers**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "of nothing" (literal translation)
> ✓ CORRECT: Use context appropriately
> 
> "No tengo nada" = "I don't have anything" / "I have nothing"
> Not "I have of nothing"
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 9: Capitalizing colors**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "I like Blue and Red"
> ✓ CORRECT: "I like blue and red"
> 
> Exception: Only capitalize at start of sentence
> or in titles
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 10: Wrong pronunciation of "Wednesday"**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "wed-nes-day" (three syllables)
> ✓ CORRECT: "wenz-day" (two syllables)
> 
> The first 'd' is silent in normal speech!
> ```
