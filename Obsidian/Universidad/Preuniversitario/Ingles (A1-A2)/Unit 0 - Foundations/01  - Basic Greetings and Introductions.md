# 🎯 Basic Greetings and Introductions

## 🌟 What Are Greetings and Introductions?

> [!info] Definition **Greetings and introductions are the first words we use when we meet someone or start a conversation. They are essential for polite communication and creating connections with others. Mastering basic greetings is your first step to speaking English confidently in social situations.**
> 
> **Key points:**
> 
> - **Greetings** = words or phrases to acknowledge someone
> - **Introductions** = telling someone your name or presenting yourself
> - Different levels of **formality** (formal vs. informal)
> - Context matters: **time of day**, **relationship**, **setting**
> - Cultural differences: English greetings vary by country
> - Body language is important: **smile**, **eye contact**, **handshake**

### 📖 Cultural Context

> [!note]- Greetings Across English-Speaking Cultures
> 
> **United States:**
> 
> - Friendly and informal in most situations
> - Common to ask "How are you?" (often not expecting a detailed answer)
> - Handshakes common in business; hugs among friends
> - First names used quickly, even in professional settings
> 
> **United Kingdom:**
> 
> - More formal, especially with strangers
> - "How do you do?" is formal; "How are you?" is casual
> - Handshakes standard; less physical contact than US
> - Titles (Mr., Mrs., Dr.) used more often initially
> 
> **Australia:**
> 
> - Very casual and friendly
> - "G'day" and "mate" are common informal greetings
> - Relaxed even in professional settings
> - Direct and straightforward communication style
> 
> **Important cultural notes:**
> 
> - In Spanish-speaking cultures, "besos" (cheek kisses) are common
> - In English-speaking cultures, this is less common (mainly among close friends/family)
> - Professional settings: stick to handshakes
> - When unsure: follow the other person's lead

## 👋 Basic Greetings

### 🕐 Greetings by Time of Day

> [!success]- Formal Time-Based Greetings
> 
> **Good morning** /ɡʊd ˈmɔːrnɪŋ/
> 
> ```
> Use: From sunrise until approximately 12:00 PM (noon)
> Context: Any situation (formal or informal)
> 
> Examples:
> • "Good morning, Mr. Smith."
> • "Good morning! How are you today?"
> • "Good morning, class!"
> 
> Response: "Good morning!" (repeat the greeting)
> 
> Spanish equivalent: "Buenos días"
> ```
> 
> **Good afternoon** /ɡʊd ˌæftərˈnuːn/
> 
> ```
> Use: From 12:00 PM until approximately 5:00-6:00 PM
> Context: Any situation (formal or informal)
> 
> Examples:
> • "Good afternoon, everyone."
> • "Good afternoon! Beautiful day, isn't it?"
> • "Good afternoon, Dr. Johnson."
> 
> Response: "Good afternoon!"
> 
> Spanish equivalent: "Buenas tardes"
> ```
> 
> **Good evening** /ɡʊd ˈiːvnɪŋ/
> 
> ```
> Use: From approximately 5:00-6:00 PM until bedtime
> Context: More formal than "good night"
> 
> Examples:
> • "Good evening, ladies and gentlemen."
> • "Good evening! Thanks for coming."
> • "Good evening, officer."
> 
> Response: "Good evening!"
> 
> Spanish equivalent: "Buenas noches" (for greeting)
> ```
> 
> **Good night** /ɡʊd ˈnaɪt/
> 
> ```
> ⚠️ IMPORTANT: "Good night" is NOT a greeting!
> 
> Use: Only for saying GOODBYE or before going to sleep
> Context: End of the day/evening
> 
> Examples:
> • "Good night! Sleep well."
> • "I'm going to bed. Good night!"
> • "Thanks for dinner. Good night!"
> 
> ❌ WRONG: "Good night!" (when arriving somewhere)
> ✓ CORRECT: "Good evening!" (when arriving in the evening)
> 
> Spanish equivalent: "Buenas noches" (for goodbye)
> 
> Note: Spanish "Buenas noches" is used for BOTH greeting and goodbye,
> but English separates them: "Good evening" (hello) vs "Good night" (goodbye)
> ```

### 💬 General Greetings (Any Time)

> [!important]- Informal and Casual Greetings
> 
> **Hello** /həˈloʊ/ or /hɛˈloʊ/
> 
> ```
> Formality: Neutral (can be formal or informal)
> Use: Any time of day, any situation
> Most universal and safe greeting
> 
> Examples:
> • "Hello! Nice to meet you."
> • "Hello, how can I help you?"
> • "Hello there!"
> 
> Spanish equivalent: "Hola"
> ```
> 
> **Hi** /haɪ/
> 
> ```
> Formality: Informal/casual
> Use: With friends, family, colleagues you know well
> Very common in everyday conversation
> 
> Examples:
> • "Hi! How's it going?"
> • "Hi there!"
> • "Hi, John!"
> 
> ⚠️ Avoid with: bosses, customers, formal situations
> 
> Spanish equivalent: "Hola" (casual)
> ```
> 
> **Hey** /heɪ/
> 
> ```
> Formality: Very informal/casual
> Use: Only with friends, peers, close colleagues
> Common among young people
> 
> Examples:
> • "Hey! What's up?"
> • "Hey, how are you?"
> • "Hey guys!"
> 
> ⚠️ DO NOT use with: superiors, strangers, formal settings
> Can sound rude if used inappropriately
> 
> Spanish equivalent: "¡Ey!" or "¿Qué tal?"
> ```
> 
> **Howdy** /ˈhaʊdi/
> 
> ```
> Formality: Very informal/regional
> Use: Mainly in Southern/Western United States
> Fun and friendly but limited usage
> 
> Examples:
> • "Howdy, partner!"
> • "Howdy, folks!"
> 
> Origin: Shortened form of "How do you do?"
> Spanish equivalent: Similar to "¿Qué tal?"
> ```
> 
> **What's up?** /wʌts ʌp/
> 
> ```
> Formality: Very informal/slang
> Use: Only with friends and peers
> Common response: "Not much" or "Nothing much"
> 
> Examples:
> • "What's up, man?"
> • "Hey! What's up?"
> 
> Variations:
> • "Sup?" /sʌp/ (very casual, shortened form)
> • "Wassup?" /wʌsʌp/ (slang)
> 
> ⚠️ This is NOT a real question expecting a detailed answer!
> It's just a casual greeting like "hi"
> 
> Spanish equivalent: "¿Qué onda?" / "¿Qué hay?"
> ```

### 👥 Asking "How Are You?"

> [!important]- Different Ways to Ask About Someone's Well-being
> 
> **How are you?** /haʊ ɑːr juː/
> 
> ```
> Formality: Neutral (formal or informal)
> Use: Most common way to ask about someone's condition
> 
> Standard responses:
> ✓ "I'm fine, thank you. And you?"
> ✓ "I'm good, thanks. How about you?"
> ✓ "I'm well, thank you."
> ✓ "Great! And you?"
> ✓ "Not bad. You?"
> 
> ⚠️ Cultural note: In English, this is often just a greeting,
> not a deep question about your health or feelings.
> Keep your answer short and positive.
> 
> Spanish equivalent: "¿Cómo estás?"
> ```
> 
> **How are you doing?** /haʊ ɑːr juː ˈduːɪŋ/
> 
> ```
> Formality: Informal to neutral
> Use: Slightly more casual than "How are you?"
> More common in American English
> 
> Responses:
> ✓ "I'm doing well, thanks!"
> ✓ "Doing great! You?"
> ✓ "Pretty good, how about you?"
> 
> Spanish equivalent: "¿Cómo te va?"
> ```
> 
> **How's it going?** /haʊz ɪt ˈɡoʊɪŋ/
> 
> ```
> Formality: Informal
> Use: Casual conversation with friends or colleagues
> 
> Responses:
> ✓ "It's going well, thanks!"
> ✓ "Good! You?"
> ✓ "Not bad! How about you?"
> 
> Spanish equivalent: "¿Cómo va?"
> ```
> 
> **How have you been?** /haʊ hæv juː bɪn/
> 
> ```
> Use: When you haven't seen someone for a while
> Implies time has passed since you last met
> 
> Responses:
> ✓ "I've been good, thanks! How about you?"
> ✓ "I've been great! Busy with work. You?"
> ✓ "Pretty good! Long time no see!"
> 
> Spanish equivalent: "¿Cómo has estado?"
> ```
> 
> **What's up?** / **What's new?** / **How's everything?**
> 
> ```
> Formality: Informal
> Use: Very casual, between friends
> 
> Responses:
> ✓ "Not much, you?"
> ✓ "Same old, same old."
> ✓ "Nothing much. What about you?"
> ✓ "Everything's good!"
> 
> Spanish equivalent: "¿Qué hay de nuevo?" / "¿Qué cuentas?"
> ```

## 🤝 Introductions

### 👤 Introducing Yourself

> [!success]- How to Introduce Yourself Properly
> 
> **Basic self-introduction structure:**
> 
> ```
> 1. Greeting
> 2. Name
> 3. Optional: Additional information (where you're from, occupation, etc.)
> 4. Polite closing phrase
> ```
> 
> **Formal introductions:**
> 
> ```
> Pattern 1:
> "Hello, my name is [Full Name]."
> "Nice to meet you."
> 
> Example:
> "Hello, my name is María García. Nice to meet you."
> 
> Pattern 2:
> "Good morning/afternoon. I'm [First Name Last Name]."
> 
> Example:
> "Good afternoon. I'm Carlos Rodríguez."
> 
> Pattern 3 (very formal):
> "How do you do? I'm [Title + Last Name]."
> 
> Example:
> "How do you do? I'm Dr. Martinez."
> ```
> 
> **Informal introductions:**
> 
> ```
> Pattern 1:
> "Hi, I'm [First Name]."
> 
> Example:
> "Hi, I'm Ana."
> 
> Pattern 2:
> "Hey! My name's [First Name]."
> 
> Example:
> "Hey! My name's Pedro."
> 
> Pattern 3 (very casual):
> "I'm [First Name]. What's your name?"
> 
> Example:
> "I'm Luis. What's your name?"
> ```
> 
> **Extended introductions (with more information):**
> 
> ```
> Professional setting:
> "Hello, my name is [Name]. I'm a [job/position] at [company/place].
> It's a pleasure to meet you."
> 
> Example:
> "Hello, my name is Sofia Mendez. I'm a teacher at Lincoln High School.
> It's a pleasure to meet you."
> 
> Social setting:
> "Hi, I'm [Name]. I'm from [place/country].
> Nice to meet you!"
> 
> Example:
> "Hi, I'm Diego. I'm from Ecuador.
> Nice to meet you!"
> 
> Academic setting:
> "Good morning. My name is [Name] and I'm studying [subject/major]
> at [university/school]."
> 
> Example:
> "Good morning. My name is Laura Vega and I'm studying engineering
> at the University of Guayaquil."
> ```

### 🔄 Asking Someone's Name

> [!important]- Different Ways to Ask for a Name
> 
> **Formal ways:**
> 
> ```
> "What is your name?"
> /wʌt ɪz jɔːr neɪm/
> 
> • Most common and neutral
> • Safe in any situation
> 
> "May I have your name, please?"
> • Very polite
> • Used in professional settings
> 
> "Could I ask your name?"
> • Very polite
> • Shows respect
> ```
> 
> **Informal ways:**
> 
> ```
> "What's your name?"
> /wʌts jɔːr neɪm/
> 
> • Casual, everyday use
> • Shortened form of "What is your name?"
> 
> "And you are...?"
> • Implies expectation that they'll introduce themselves
> • Slightly informal
> 
> "I didn't catch your name."
> • When you didn't hear or forgot their name
> • Polite way to ask again
> ```
> 
> **Responses:**
> 
> ```
> Formal:
> ✓ "My name is [Full Name]."
> ✓ "I'm [First Name Last Name]."
> 
> Informal:
> ✓ "I'm [First Name]."
> ✓ "My name's [First Name]."
> ✓ "[First Name]." (just the name)
> 
> Asking them back:
> ✓ "My name is Ana. And yours?"
> ✓ "I'm Carlos. What's your name?"
> ✓ "I'm María. And you?"
> ```

### 👥 Introducing Others

> [!success]- How to Introduce Two People
> 
> **Formal introductions:**
> 
> ```
> Pattern:
> "[Name 1], this is [Name 2]."
> "[Name 2], this is [Name 1]."
> 
> Example:
> "Mr. Johnson, this is Ms. García, our new manager."
> "Ms. García, this is Mr. Johnson, our director."
> 
> Alternative formal pattern:
> "I'd like to introduce you to [Name]."
> "May I introduce [Name]?"
> 
> Example:
> "I'd like to introduce you to Dr. Smith."
> ```
> 
> **Informal introductions:**
> 
> ```
> Pattern:
> "[Name 1], meet [Name 2]."
> "[Name 2], [Name 1]."
> 
> Example:
> "Sarah, meet my friend Tom."
> "Tom, this is Sarah from work."
> 
> Very casual:
> "This is [Name]."
> 
> Example:
> "Hey guys, this is Miguel."
> ```
> 
> **Adding context (recommended):**
> 
> ```
> Include HOW you know each person:
> 
> "John, this is Maria. She's my colleague from the marketing department."
> "Maria, this is John. He's an old friend from university."
> 
> "Dad, this is my friend Carlos. We go to school together."
> "Carlos, this is my father."
> 
> This helps people find common ground for conversation!
> ```

>

> ```

### 😊 Excuse Me and Sorry

> [!warning]- When and How to Apologize
> 
> **"Excuse me" /ɪkˈskjuːz miː/**
> 
> ```
> Use BEFORE doing something:
> 
> ✓ To get someone's attention:
>   "Excuse me, do you have the time?"
> 
> ✓ To pass through a crowd:
>   "Excuse me, may I get through?"
> 
> ✓ To interrupt politely:
>   "Excuse me, may I ask a question?"
> 
> ✓ To ask for something:
>   "Excuse me, where is the bathroom?"
> 
> Spanish equivalent: "Disculpe" / "Perdón" (before action)
> ```
> 
> **"I'm sorry" /aɪm ˈsɔːri/**
> 
> ```
> Use AFTER making a mistake or doing something wrong:
> 
> ✓ For actual mistakes:
>   "I'm sorry, I didn't mean to step on your foot."
> 
> ✓ For being late:
>   "I'm sorry I'm late."
> 
> ✓ To express sympathy:
>   "I'm sorry to hear that."
> 
> Levels of apology:
> • "Sorry." (casual)
> • "I'm sorry." (standard)
> • "I'm very sorry." (more serious)
> • "I'm terribly sorry." (formal, serious)
> • "I apologize." (formal)
> 
> Responses to apology:
> ✓ "That's okay."
> ✓ "No worries."
> ✓ "It's fine."
> ✓ "Don't worry about it."
> 
> Spanish equivalent: "Lo siento" / "Perdón" (after action)
> ```
> 
> **Key difference:**
> 
> ```
> ❌ Common mistake for Spanish speakers:
> Using "sorry" when you should use "excuse me"
> 
> ✓ CORRECT: "Excuse me, where is the bank?"
> ❌ WRONG: "Sorry, where is the bank?"
> 
> In Spanish, "perdón" works for both situations,
> but English separates them!
> ```

## 👋 Saying Goodbye

### 🚪 Farewell Expressions

> [!success]- Different Ways to Say Goodbye
> 
> **Formal farewells:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ "Goodbye." /ˌɡʊdˈbaɪ/
>   • Standard, any situation
>   • Most neutral farewell
> 
> ✓ "Good night."
>   • Only when parting in the evening/night
>   • Or before going to sleep
> 
> ✓ "Have a good day."
>   • Polite, wishing them well
>   • Common in service situations
> 
> ✓ "Have a nice evening."
>   • In the evening
> 
> ✓ "It was nice meeting you."
>   • After first meeting someone
> 
> ✓ "Take care."
>   • Warm, showing you care about them
> 
> Spanish equivalent: "Adiós" / "Que tenga buen día"
> ```
> 
> **Informal farewells:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ "Bye!" /baɪ/
>   • Most common casual goodbye
> 
> ✓ "Bye-bye!"
>   • Casual, sometimes used with children
> 
> ✓ "See you!" / "See ya!"
>   • When you'll see them again
> 
> ✓ "See you later!"
>   • Implies you'll meet again soon
> 
> ✓ "See you tomorrow!"
>   • When you'll see them the next day
> 
> ✓ "Catch you later!"
>   • Very informal, between friends
> 
> ✓ "Take it easy."
>   • Casual, "relax and enjoy"
> 
> ✓ "Talk to you later."
>   • Often shortened to "Talk later!"
> 
> Spanish equivalent: "Chao" / "Nos vemos"
> ```
> 
> **Temporary farewells:**
> 
> ```
> When leaving briefly:
> 
> ✓ "I'll be right back."
> ✓ "Excuse me for a moment."
> ✓ "I'll be back in a minute."
> ✓ "Just a second."
> 
> Spanish equivalent: "Ya vuelvo" / "Ahora regreso"
> ```

## 📝 Common Mistakes to Avoid

> [!warning]- Typical Errors for Spanish Speakers
> 
> **Mistake 1: Using "Good night" as a greeting**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "Good night!" (when arriving in the evening)
> ✓ CORRECT: "Good evening!" (when arriving)
> ✓ CORRECT: "Good night!" (when leaving)
> 
> Explanation:
> In Spanish, "Buenas noches" is used for both greeting and goodbye.
> In English, they're different:
> • "Good evening" = greeting (arriving)
> • "Good night" = farewell (leaving)
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 2: Confusing "meet" and "see"**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "Nice to meet you again."
> ✓ CORRECT: "Nice to see you again."
> 
> ❌ WRONG: "It was good to see you." (first time meeting)
> ✓ CORRECT: "It was nice to meet you."
> 
> Rule:
> • "Meet" = first time
> • "See" = not the first time
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 3: Using "Sorry" instead of "Excuse me"**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "Sorry, where is the bathroom?"
> ✓ CORRECT: "Excuse me, where is the bathroom?"
> 
> ❌ WRONG: "Sorry, can I pass?"
> ✓ CORRECT: "Excuse me, can I pass?"
> 
> Remember:
> • "Excuse me" = BEFORE action (getting attention, asking)
> • "Sorry" = AFTER mistake (apologizing)
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 4: Giving too much information to "How are you?"**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "I'm fine, but yesterday I had a headache and
>           today I woke up late and missed the bus..."
> 
> ✓ CORRECT: "I'm fine, thanks. And you?"
> ✓ CORRECT: "I'm great! How about you?"
> 
> Note: "How are you?" is usually just a polite greeting,
> not a request for detailed information.
> Keep your answer brief and positive!
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 5: Translating "Mucho gusto" literally**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "Much pleasure."
> ❌ WRONG: "Very pleasure."
> ✓ CORRECT: "Nice to meet you."
> ✓ CORRECT: "Pleased to meet you."
> 
> Don't translate word-for-word!
> ```
> 
> **Mistake 6: Wrong word order in questions**
> 
> ```
> ❌ WRONG: "What your name is?"
> ✓ CORRECT: "What is your name?"
> 
> ❌ WRONG: "How you are?"

## 💡 Cultural Tips

> [!tip]- Important Cultural Considerations
> 
> **1. Personal Space:**
> 
> ```
> United States/UK:
> • Maintain arm's length distance
> • Handshakes are standard
> • Avoid excessive physical contact with strangers
> 
> Latin America vs. English-speaking countries:
> • Less physical contact in English cultures
> • No "besos" (cheek kisses) with strangers
> • Hugs mainly for close friends/family
> ```
> 
> **2. Eye Contact:**
> 
> ```
> English-speaking cultures:
> • Direct eye contact = honesty and confidence
> • Looking away = shyness or dishonesty
> • Maintain eye contact during greetings
> 
> This differs from some cultures where
> direct eye contact might be considered rude.
> ```
> 
> **3. Small Talk:**
> 
> ```
> "How are you?" is often just a greeting:
> 
> ✓ DO: Keep it brief and positive
>   "I'm fine, thanks!"
> 
> ✗ DON'T: Give detailed health information
>   "Well, my back hurts and I didn't sleep well..."
> 
> Safe small talk topics:
> • Weather ("Nice day, isn't it?")
> • Weekend plans
> • General work/school
> 
> Topics to avoid with strangers:
> • Politics
> • Religion
> • Money/salary
> • Age
> • Personal health problems
> ```
> 
> **4. Formality Levels:**
> 
> ```
> When in doubt, start formal!
> 
> You can always become more casual,
> but starting too casual can seem rude.
> 
> Formal → Informal transition:
> • Wait for the other person to suggest first names
> • Follow their lead in conversation style
> • Mirror their level of formality
> ```
> 
> **5. Names and Titles:**
> 
> ```
> First meeting:
> • Use titles: Mr., Mrs., Ms., Dr., Professor
> • Wait to be told "Please, call me [first name]"
> 
> Professional settings:
> • Use last names initially
> • US: often switches to first names quickly
> • UK: may stay formal longer
> 
> Note: In English, "Señor/Señora/Señorita" don't translate
> to everyday use. Use "Mr./Mrs./Ms." + last name.
> ```

> [!quote]- Enlaces Conceptuales
> 
> **📜 Fundación previa:**
> 
> **[[02 - English Alphabet and Pronunciation]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión: El alfabeto y la pronunciación son esenciales para:

> • Pronunciar correctamente los saludos
> • Entender la diferencia entre sonidos similares:
>   - "meet" /miːt/ vs "meat" /miːt/ (same sound)
>   - "see" /siː/ vs "sea" /siː/ (same sound)
> • Reconocer que "How are you?" tiene pronunciación específica
> • Los sonidos /θ/ (think) y /ð/ (this) aparecen en "thanks", "that"
> 
> Aplicación directa:
> - Thank you = /θæŋk juː/ (usar el sonido TH correctamente)
> - This, that, the = /ð/ (TH sonoro)
> - Nice to meet you = pronunciación correcta de cada palabra
> ```
> 
> **📜 Continuación inmediata:**
> 
> **[[03 - Numbers, Days, Months, Colors]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión: Después de saludar e introducirte, necesitas:
> 
> • Dar tu número de teléfono
> • Mencionar fechas (cumpleaños, citas)
> • Describir cosas básicas usando colores
> • Programar reuniones (días y horarios)
> 
> Ejemplo de progresión natural:
> "Hi, I'm Ana. Nice to meet you!"
> → "My phone number is..."
> → "My birthday is on..."
> → "Let's meet on Monday"
> ```
> 
> **[[04 - Verb _to be_]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión: Muchos saludos e introducciones usan el verbo "to be":
> 
> • "I am [name]" / "I'm [name]"
> • "How are you?"
> • "This is [person]"
> • "It is nice to meet you"
> 
> Los saludos te preparan para entender:
> - Contracciones: I'm, you're, it's
> - Preguntas: Are you...? Is this...?
> - Formas afirmativa, negativa, interrogativa
> ```
> 
> **[[05 - Personal and Possessive Pronouns]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión: Las introducciones usan muchos pronombres:
> 
> • "I'm Carlos" (I = yo)
> • "Nice to meet you" (you = tú/usted)
> • "This is my friend" (my = mi)
> • "What's your name?" (your = tu)
> 
> Foundation para:
> - Subject pronouns: I, you, he, she, it, we, they
> - Possessive adjectives: my, your, his, her, its, our, their
> ```
> 
> **[[13 - Parts of Speech (POS)]]**
> 
> ```
> Conexión: Los saludos contienen diferentes partes de la oración:
> 
> Nouns (sustantivos):
> • "Good morning" (morning = noun)

> • "Good morning" (good = adjective)
