# 🎯 Plurals and Articles

## 🌟 What Are Plurals and Articles?

> [!info] Definition **Plurals indicate more than one person, place, thing, or idea. Articles are small words (a, an, the) that come before nouns to show whether we're talking about something specific or general. Together, they form the foundation of noun usage in English.**
> 
> **Key points:**
> 
> - **Plurals:** Most English nouns add -s or -es to show "more than one"
> - **Articles:** Three articles in English: "a", "an", "the"
> - **Definite article:** "the" (specific, known item)
> - **Indefinite articles:** "a" and "an" (general, non-specific item)
> - Understanding plurals and articles is essential for basic sentence construction
> - Rules have patterns, but many exceptions exist

### 📖 Historical Context

> [!note]- Development of Plurals and Articles
> 
> **Old English period (450-1100 AD):**
> 
> - **Complex plural system:** Multiple endings (-as, -an, -u, -en, etc.)
> - **Gender system:** Nouns had grammatical gender (masculine, feminine, neuter)
> - **Articles:** "se" (masculine), "seo" (feminine), "þæt" (neuter)
> - **Case endings:** Different forms for subject, object, possessive
> - Example: "stān" (stone) → "stānas" (stones)
> 
> **Middle English period (1100-1500 AD):**
> 
> - **Simplification begins:** Many endings reduced to -es or -s
> - **Gender system lost:** No more masculine/feminine/neuter distinctions
> - **Articles simplified:** "se", "seo", "þæt" → "the"
> - **Norman French influence:** Some French plural patterns adopted
> - Irregular plurals preserved from Old English (foot→feet, mouse→mice)
> 
> **Early Modern English (1500-1700):**
> 
> - **-s plural dominates:** Became the standard ending
> - **Indefinite article "a/an" standardized:** From Old English "ān" (one)
> - **"The" fully established:** Single form for all genders and cases
> - **Printing press standardization:** Plural spelling conventions fixed
> 
> **Modern English (1700-present):**
> 
> - **Current system stabilized:** -s/-es as primary plural marker
> - **Loan word plurals:** Foreign plurals sometimes retained (radius→radii, criterion→criteria)
> - **Irregular plurals preserved:** Historical forms maintained
> - **Articles remain simple:** Three articles only (a, an, the)
> - **Usage continues to evolve:** Some irregular plurals becoming regular

## 📝 Forming Plurals

### 🔵 Regular Plurals

> [!success]- Standard Plural Formation Rules
> 
> **Rule 1: Add -s to most nouns**
> 
> ```
> The basic rule - simply add -s:
> 
> • book → books
> • cat → cats
> • dog → dogs
> • car → cars
> • table → tables
> • house → houses
> • computer → computers
> • friend → friends
> ```
> 
> **Rule 2: Add -es after s, x, z, ch, sh**
> 
> ```
> Why? These sounds need an extra syllable for pronunciation:
> 
> -s words:
> • bus → buses /ˈbʌsɪz/
> • glass → glasses
> • kiss → kisses
> • boss → bosses
> 
> -x words:
> • box → boxes
> • fox → foxes
> • tax → taxes
> • mix → mixes
> 
> -z words:
> • quiz → quizzes (double z + es)
> • buzz → buzzes
> • topaz → topazes
> 
> -ch words:
> • church → churches
> • watch → watches
> • beach → beaches
> • lunch → lunches
> 
> -sh words:
> • dish → dishes
> • wish → wishes
> • brush → brushes
> • flash → flashes
> ```
> 
> **Rule 3: Consonant + y → change y to i + es**
> 
> ```
> If a consonant comes before -y:
> 
> • baby → babies
> • city → cities
> • family → families
> • party → parties
> • lady → ladies
> • story → stories
> • country → countries
> • puppy → puppies
> 
> BUT: If a vowel comes before -y, just add -s:
> 
> • boy → boys
> • day → days
> • key → keys
> • toy → toys
> • guy → guys
> ```
> 
> **Rule 4: Most -f and -fe → change to -ves**
> 
> ```
> Common -f/-fe words:
> 
> • knife → knives
> • wife → wives
> • life → lives
> • leaf → leaves
> • loaf → loaves
> • half → halves
> • shelf → shelves
> • wolf → wolves
> • thief → thieves
> • calf → calves
> 
> EXCEPTIONS - just add -s:
> 
> • roof → roofs
> • chief → chiefs
> • belief → beliefs
> • cliff → cliffs
> • safe → safes
> ```
> 
> **Rule 5: Most -o words → add -es**
> 
> ```
> Common -o + es words:
> 
> • tomato → tomatoes
> • potato → potatoes
> • hero → heroes
> • echo → echoes
> • torpedo → torpedoes
> 
> EXCEPTIONS - just add -s:
> 
> • photo → photos
> • piano → pianos
> • solo → solos
> • auto → autos
> • memo → memos
> • zoo → zoos
> 
> Memory tip: Musical terms usually just add -s
> (piano, solo, soprano, etc.)
> ```

### 🔴 Irregular Plurals

> [!warning]- Irregular Plural Patterns
> 
> **Type 1: Vowel change (Old English plurals)**
> 
> ```
> These are ancient forms preserved from Old English:
> 
> • man → men
> • woman → women /ˈwɪmɪn/
> • foot → feet
> • tooth → teeth
> • goose → geese
> • mouse → mice
> • louse → lice
> 
> Note: "womans" and "foots" are WRONG - never use regular -s!
> ```
> 
> **Type 2: -en plural (Old English survival)**
> 
> ```
> Very rare - only a few words:
> 
> • child → children
> • ox → oxen
> 
> Note: "childs" is WRONG!
> ```
> 
> **Type 3: Same form (zero plural)**
> 
> ```
> Animals (especially fish and hunted animals):
> 
> • sheep → sheep
> • deer → deer
> • fish → fish (or fishes for species)
> • moose → moose
> • salmon → salmon
> • trout → trout
> • bison → bison
> • shrimp → shrimp (or shrimps)
> 
> Other words:
> 
> • series → series
> • species → species
> • aircraft → aircraft
> • spacecraft → spacecraft
> • means → means
> ```
> 
> **Type 4: Latin and Greek plurals**
> 
> ```
> From Latin -us → -i:
> 
> • cactus → cacti (or cactuses)
> • radius → radii
> • alumnus → alumni
> • fungus → fungi (or funguses)
> • nucleus → nuclei
> • stimulus → stimuli
> 
> From Latin -a → -ae:
> 
> • formula → formulae (or formulas)
> • antenna → antennae (or antennas)
> • larva → larvae
> • vertebra → vertebrae
> 
> From Latin -um → -a:
> 
> • bacterium → bacteria
> • datum → data
> • medium → media
> • curriculum → curricula (or curriculums)
> • memorandum → memoranda (or memorandums)
> 
> From Greek -on → -a:
> 
> • criterion → criteria
> • phenomenon → phenomena
> 
> From Greek -is → -es:
> 
> • analysis → analyses /əˈnæləsiːz/
> • basis → bases /ˈbeɪsiːz/
> • crisis → crises /ˈkraɪsiːz/
> • thesis → theses /ˈθiːsiːz/
> • diagnosis → diagnoses
> • hypothesis → hypotheses
> 
> Note: Many of these now accept regular -s/-es in informal English
> (formulas, curriculums, etc.)
> ```
> 
> **Type 5: Compound nouns**
> 
> ```
> Make the main noun plural:
> 
> • mother-in-law → mothers-in-law
> • passer-by → passers-by
> • attorney-general → attorneys-general
> • father-in-law → fathers-in-law
> • brother-in-law → brothers-in-law
> 
> If no clear main noun, add -s at the end:
> 
> • grown-up → grown-ups
> • check-in → check-ins
> • breakdown → breakdowns
> ```

### ⚠️ Special Cases and Exceptions

> [!important]- Uncountable Nouns and Special Plurals
> 
> **Uncountable nouns (no plural form):**
> 
> ```
> These nouns don't have a plural - they're always singular:
> 
> Abstract concepts:
> • information (NOT "informations")
> • advice (NOT "advices")
> • knowledge
> • homework
> • work
> • news (looks plural but is singular!)
> • progress
> • research
> 
> Materials and substances:
> • water
> • rice
> • sugar
> • milk
> • bread
> • butter
> • gold
> • air
> 
> General categories:
> • furniture (NOT "furnitures")
> • equipment (NOT "equipments")
> • luggage/baggage (NOT "luggages")
> • money
> • traffic
> • weather
> 
> To count these, use: a piece of, some, much, a lot of
> • a piece of advice
> • some information
> • much homework
> ```
> 
> **Nouns that are always plural:**
> 
> ```
> These require plural verbs:
> 
> Clothing items:
> • pants/trousers (are, not is)
> • jeans
> • shorts
> • glasses/spectacles
> • scissors
> • pajamas
> 
> Other:
> • clothes
> • goods
> • thanks
> • congratulations
> • police (treated as plural)
> • people (treated as plural)
> 
> Example: "My pants ARE blue." (NOT "is blue")
> ```
> 
> **Plural forms with different meanings:**
> 
> ```
> • fish (same type) → fish / (different types) → fishes
> • people (general) → people / (ethnic groups) → peoples
> • work (general) → work / (artistic creations) → works
> • hair (on head) → hair / (individual strands) → hairs
> • glass (material) → glass / (drinking vessels) → glasses
> ```

## 📰 The Definite Article: "THE"

### 🎯 When to Use "THE"

> [!success]- Complete Guide to "THE"
> 
> **Rule 1: Specific, known items**
> 
> ```
> Use "the" when both speaker and listener know which specific thing:
> 
> • "Close the door." (We both know which door)
> • "The book on the table is mine." (That specific book)
> • "Did you feed the dog?" (Our dog, not any dog)
> • "The teacher was late today." (Our specific teacher)
> ```
> 
> **Rule 2: Second mention**
> 
> ```
> First mention: "I saw a cat."
> Second mention: "The cat was black."
> 
> First: "There's a new restaurant downtown."
> Second: "The restaurant serves Italian food."
> ```
> 
> **Rule 3: Unique things**
> 
> ```
> Only one exists:
> 
> • the sun
> • the moon
> • the Earth
> • the sky
> • the world
> • the universe
> • the internet
> • the environment
> ```
> 
> **Rule 4: Superlatives and ordinals**
> 
> ```
> Superlatives:
> • the best
> • the biggest
> • the most important
> • the fastest
> 
> Ordinals:
> • the first
> • the second
> • the last
> • the only
> ```
> 
> **Rule 5: Geographic features**
> 
> ```
> Oceans, seas, rivers:
> • the Pacific Ocean
> • the Atlantic
> • the Mediterranean Sea
> • the Amazon River
> • the Nile
> 
> Mountain ranges (not individual mountains):
> • the Andes
> • the Himalayas
> • the Alps
> BUT: Mount Everest (no "the")
> 
> Deserts:
> • the Sahara Desert
> • the Gobi Desert
> 
> Groups of islands:
> • the Philippines
> • the Canary Islands
> • the Bahamas
> BUT: Hawaii (no "the")
> 
> Countries with "of" or plural names:
> • the United States
> • the United Kingdom
> • the Netherlands
> • the Philippines
> • the Republic of Ecuador
> ```
> 
> **Rule 6: Musical instruments**
> 
> ```
> When talking about playing:
> 
> • "I play the piano."
> • "She plays the guitar."
> • "He plays the violin."
> • "They play the drums."
> ```
> 
> **Rule 7: Newspapers, organizations, famous buildings**
> 
> ```
> Newspapers:
> • the New York Times
> • the Guardian
> • the Washington Post
> 
> Organizations:
> • the United Nations (UN)
> • the European Union (EU)
> • the Red Cross
> 
> Buildings/monuments:
> • the White House
> • the Eiffel Tower
> • the Empire State Building
> ```

### 🚫 When NOT to Use "THE"

> [!warning]- When to Omit "THE"
> 
> **No "the" with:**
> 
> **1. General plural nouns:**
> 
> ```
> • "I like dogs." (dogs in general)
> • "Cats are independent." (all cats)
> • "Books are expensive." (books in general)
> • "Children need love." (children in general)
> 
> Compare:
> ✗ "The dogs are friendly." (specific dogs we know)
> ✓ "Dogs are friendly." (dogs in general)
> ```
> 
> **2. Uncountable nouns (general meaning):**
> 
> ```
> • "I need advice." (advice in general)
> • "Love is important." (love as a concept)
> • "Water is essential." (water in general)
> • "Music makes me happy." (music in general)
> 
> BUT use "the" when specific:
> • "The water in this bottle is cold." (specific water)
> • "The music at the party was loud." (specific music)
> ```
> 
> **3. Most countries, cities, streets:**
> 
> ```
> Countries:
> • Ecuador (NOT "the Ecuador")
> • Spain
> • France
> • Japan
> • China
> 
> Cities:
> • Guayaquil
> • New York
> • London
> • Paris
> 
> Streets:
> • Main Street
> • Fifth Avenue
> • Oxford Street
> ```
> 
> **4. Languages:**
> 
> ```
> • "I speak English." (NOT "the English")
> • "She studies Spanish."
> • "They learn French."
> 
> BUT: "The English language" (when "language" is included)
> ```
> 
> **5. Sports and academic subjects:**
> 
> ```
> Sports:
> • "I play soccer." (NOT "the soccer")
> • "He likes basketball."
> • "They play tennis."
> 
> Subjects:
> • "I study mathematics."
> • "She teaches history."
> • "He likes chemistry."
> ```
> 
> **6. Meals:**
> 
> ```
> • "I had breakfast." (NOT "the breakfast")
> • "Lunch is ready."
> • "What's for dinner?"
> 
> BUT use "the" when specific:
> • "The breakfast we had yesterday was delicious."
> ```
> 
> **7. Possessives:**
> 
> ```
> • "My car is red." (NOT "the my car")
> • "Her house is big."
> • "Our teacher is kind."
> ```

## 🔤 Indefinite Articles: "A" and "AN"

### 📌 Basic Rules for A/AN

> [!info]- Understanding A and AN
> 
> **The fundamental rule:**
> 
> ```
> Use "a" or "an" for:
> • ONE non-specific item
> • Something mentioned for the FIRST time
> • Singular countable nouns only
> 
> "A" vs "AN" depends on SOUND, not spelling!
> ```
> 
> **Use "A" before consonant SOUNDS:**
> 
> ```
> • a book
> • a car
> • a dog
> • a house
> • a table
> • a university (starts with /j/ sound: "yoo-ni-ver-si-ty")
> • a European country (starts with /j/ sound)
> • a one-way street (starts with /w/ sound)
> • a useful tool (starts with /j/ sound)
> ```
> 
> **Use "AN" before vowel SOUNDS:**
> 
> ```
> • an apple
> • an egg
> • an idea
> • an orange
> • an umbrella
> • an hour (silent H: starts with /aʊ/ sound)
> • an honest person (silent H)
> • an honor (silent H)
> • an FBI agent (F = /ef/ starts with vowel sound)
> • an MBA degree (M = /em/ starts with vowel sound)
> ```

### 🎯 When to Use A/AN

> [!success]- Complete A/AN Usage Guide
> 
> **1. First mention (introducing something new):**
> 
> ```
> • "I saw a cat in the garden."
> • "She bought an umbrella."
> • "There's a problem."
> • "He's reading a book."
> ```
> 
> **2. Jobs and professions:**
> 
> ```
> • "I'm a teacher."
> • "She's a doctor."
> • "He's an engineer."
> • "They're artists." (no article in plural)
> • "My father is a lawyer."
> ```
> 
> **3. Classifying or describing:**
> 
> ```
> • "That's a beautiful house."
> • "It's an old car."
> • "This is a difficult question."
> • "She's an intelligent student."
> ```
> 
> **4. Meaning "one":**
> 
> ```
> • "I need a minute." (one minute)
> • "It costs a dollar." (one dollar)
> • "Wait a second." (one second)
> • "I have a sister." (one sister)
> ```
> 
> **5. In exclamations with "what":**
> 
> ```
> • "What a beautiful day!"
> • "What an incredible story!"
> • "What a surprise!"
> • "What an amazing view!"
> ```
> 
> **6. Expressions with frequency:**
> 
> ```
> • "once a day"
> • "twice a week"
> • "three times a month"
> • "60 miles an hour"
> • "$10 a kilo"
> ```

### 🚫 When NOT to Use A/AN

> [!warning]- When to Omit A/AN
> 
> **1. With plural nouns:**
> 
> ```
> ✗ "I have a books." → ✓ "I have books."
> ✗ "She bought a shoes." → ✓ "She bought shoes."
> ✗ "There are a cats." → ✓ "There are cats."
> ```
> 
> **2. With uncountable nouns:**
> 
> ```
> ✗ "I need a water." → ✓ "I need water." or "I need some water."
> ✗ "She gave me a advice." → ✓ "She gave me advice."
> ✗ "He has a money." → ✓ "He has money."
> ✗ "We need a information." → ✓ "We need information."
> ```
> 
> **3. With possessives:**
> 
> ```
> ✗ "That's a my book." → ✓ "That's my book."
> ✗ "This is a her car." → ✓ "This is her car."
> ```
> 
> **4. With demonstratives:**
> 
> ```
> ✗ "I want a this one." → ✓ "I want this one."
> ✗ "Give me a that book." → ✓ "Give me that book."
> ```

> ```
> 
> **Conexión práctica:**
> 
> ```
> Estos tres elementos forman la base de oraciones básicas:
> 
> ARTÍCULO + SUSTANTIVO + VERBO
> "A cat runs"
> "The cats run"
> 
> Sin dominar plurales y artículos, no puedes:
> • Construir oraciones correctamente
> • Concordar sujeto-verbo
> • Hablar de cantidades
> • Distinguir entre específico y general
> ```

## 💡 Common Mistakes and Tips

> [!warning]- Errores Comunes para Hispanohablantes
> 
> **Error 1: Usar "the" con nombres de países**
> 
> ```
> ✗ "I'm from the Ecuador."
> ✓ "I'm from Ecuador."
> 
> ✗ "The Spain is beautiful."
> ✓ "Spain is beautiful."
> 
> EXCEPCIÓN: the United States, the Netherlands, the Philippines
> (cuando el nombre tiene "of" o es plural)
> ```
> 
> **Error 2: Plurales dobles (influencia del español)**
> 
> ```
> En español: "los libros" (artículo + plural)
> En inglés: solo el sustantivo tiene plural
> 
> ✗ "the books are goods"
> ✓ "the books are good"
> 
> ✗ "I have two dogs blacks."
> ✓ "I have two black dogs."
> ```
> 
> **Error 3: Usar "a/an" con incontables**
> 
> ```
> ✗ "I need a water."
> ✓ "I need water." / "I need some water."
> 
> ✗ "She gave me a information."
> ✓ "She gave me information." / "She gave me some information."
> 
> ✗ "Can I have a bread?"
> ✓ "Can I have some bread?" / "Can I have a piece of bread?"
> ```
> 
> **Error 4: Omitir artículos cuando son necesarios**
> 
> ```
> En español: "Soy profesor" (sin artículo)
> En inglés: necesitas "a/an"
> 
> ✗ "I'm teacher."
> ✓ "I'm a teacher."
> 
> ✗ "She's doctor."
> ✓ "She's a doctor."
> 
> ✗ "This is good book."
> ✓ "This is a good book."
> ```
> 
> **Error 5: Confundir A/AN por la letra, no el sonido**
> 
> ```
> ✗ "a hour" (porque H es consonante)
> ✓ "an hour" (porque H es muda, suena /aʊər/)
> 
> ✗ "an university" (porque U es vocal)
> ✓ "a university" (porque suena /juː/)
> 
> Recuerda: El SONIDO determina A o AN, no la letra escrita
> ```
> 
> **Error 6: Plurales irregulares comunes**
> 
> ```
> ✗ "childs" → ✓ "children"
> ✗ "mans" → ✓ "men"
> ✗ "womans" → ✓ "women"
> ✗ "foots" → ✓ "feet"
> ✗ "tooths" → ✓ "teeth"
> ✗ "mouses" → ✓ "mice"
> ✗ "sheeps" → ✓ "sheep"
> ✗ "persons" → ✓ "people" (más común)
> ```
> 
> **Error 7: Usar "the" con conceptos generales**
> 
> ```
> ✗ "The life is beautiful." (vida en general)
> ✓ "Life is beautiful."
> 
> ✗ "The people are friendly." (gente en general)
> ✓ "People are friendly."
> 
> ✗ "I love the music." (música en general)
> ✓ "I love music."
> 
> PERO: "The music at the party was great." (música específica)
> ```

> [!tip]- Consejos y Trucos de Memoria
> 
> **Tip 1: Regla del sonido para A/AN**
> 
> ```
> Truco de memoria:
> "AN" = vowel SOUND starts with A, E, I, O, U sounds
> "A" = everything else
> 
> Practica diciendo la palabra en voz alta:
> • "university" → /juː/ → suena como "you" → A university
> • "hour" → /aʊ/ → suena como "our" → AN hour
> • "honest" → /ɑː/ → H muda → AN honest
> ```
> 
> **Tip 2: Para recordar plurales irregulares**
> 
> ```
> Grupo 1 - Cambio de vocal (como en español):
> man → men (como "man → men")
> foot → feet (como "pie → pies" pero con vocal)
> 
> Grupo 2 - Igual que singular:
> sheep → sheep
> deer → deer
> fish → fish
> Piensa: "Los animales de granja/caza no cambian"
> 
> Grupo 3 - Solo dos palabras con -en:
> child → children
> ox → oxen
> ¡Solo memoriza estos dos!
> ```
> 
> **Tip 3: THE con cosas únicas**
> 
> ```
> Si solo hay UNO en el mundo → usa THE:
> • the sun (solo hay un sol)
> • the moon (solo hay una luna)
> • the internet (solo hay un internet)
> • the president (de tu país, solo hay uno)
> 
> Pero: a president (en general, hay muchos presidentes en el mundo)
> ```
> 
> **Tip 4: Para incontables comunes**
> 
> ```
> Memoriza esta lista (NO usan A/AN, NO tienen plural):
> 
> Líquidos: water, milk, coffee, tea, juice
> Comidas: bread, rice, sugar, meat, cheese
> Materiales: wood, gold, paper, glass
> Abstractos: love, happiness, information, advice, news, homework
> Otros: money, furniture, luggage, weather, traffic
> 
> Para contar: use "a piece of", "some", "a cup of", "a glass of"
> • a piece of advice
> • a glass of water
> • a piece of furniture
> ```
> 
> **Tip 5: Plural en -y**
> 
> ```
> Regla fácil:
> VOCAL + Y → solo agrega -S
> • boy → boys
> • day → days
> • key → keys
> 
> CONSONANTE + Y → cambia Y a I, agrega -ES
> • baby → babies
> • city → cities
> • party → parties
> 
> Truco: Si puedes "escuchar" la Y como /ai/ o /ei/, solo agrega -S
> ```
> 
> **Tip 6: Primera mención vs Segunda mención**
> 
> ```
> Historia simple:
> "Yesterday I saw A dog. THE dog was big."
> 
> Primera vez = nuevo = A/AN
> Segunda vez = ya lo conocemos = THE
> 
> Piensa: A = presentando algo nuevo
>         THE = ya lo conocemos
> ```

## 🎓 Summary - Resumen

> [!summary]- Key Points to Remember
> 
> **Plurals - Puntos Clave:**
> 
> ```
> ✓ Regular: add -s (book → books)
> ✓ After s,x,z,ch,sh: add -es (box → boxes)
> ✓ Consonant + y: change to -ies (baby → babies)
> ✓ Vowel + y: just add -s (boy → boys)
> ✓ Most f/fe: change to -ves (knife → knives)
> ✓ Irregular: memorize (man→men, child→children, sheep→sheep)
> ✓ Uncountable nouns: NO plural form (information, advice, water)
> ```
> 
> **Articles - Puntos Clave:**
> 
> ```
> A/AN (indefinite):
> ✓ Use with singular countable nouns
> ✓ First mention / non-specific
> ✓ A = consonant SOUND
> ✓ AN = vowel SOUND
> ✓ Cannot use with plurals or uncountables
> 
> THE (definite):
> ✓ Specific / known item
> ✓ Second mention
> ✓ Unique things (the sun, the moon)
> ✓ Superlatives (the best)
> ✓ Musical instruments (play the piano)
> 
> NO ARTICLE (Ø):
> ✓ General plurals (Cats are nice)
> ✓ Uncountables general (Water is essential)
> ✓ Most countries, cities (Ecuador, Guayaquil)
> ✓ Languages (I speak English)
> ✓ Sports and subjects (play soccer, study math)
> ```
> 
> **Para hispanohablantes - Errores críticos a evitar:**
> 
> ```
> ✗ the Ecuador → ✓ Ecuador
> ✗ I'm teacher → ✓ I'm a teacher
> ✗ a water → ✓ water / some water
> ✗ childs → ✓ children
> ✗ a informations → ✓ information
> ✗ The life is beautiful → ✓ Life is beautiful
> ✗ I play the soccer → ✓ I play soccer
> ```
